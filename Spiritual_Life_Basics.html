Introduction

The Christian life operates on two distinct but inseparable levels: the content of the faith, and the manner in which that faith is to be lived. The Greek noun pistis (πίστις) carries both meanings. It names the act of believing — the subjective response of the soul to the gospel. It also names the body of doctrine believed — “the faith” as an objective deposit, the revealed counsel of God. Paul’s declaration in Romans 1:16–17 turns on this double sense: the righteousness of God is revealed ek pisteos eis pistin (ἐκ πίστεως εἰς πίστιν) — from faith to faith. The salvation adjustment, faith alone in Christ alone, is the beginning. What follows is a lifetime of faith as the operational mode of the soul: doctrine received, metabolized, and applied to the realities of life in time.

These two levels are not the same thing, and conflating them produces specific and identifiable consequences. Much Christian teaching concerns the content of the faith — the Person and Work of Jesus Christ, the plan of God, the promise of eternal life. This is not optional; it is foundational. No one can live what he does not know. But doctrine that remains in the left lobe as academic knowledge — gnosis — without transfer to the right lobe as metabolized certainty — epignosis — produces no spiritual fruit. A believer can hold every fact of systematic theology in the left lobe and still live in spiritual defeat, because the content of the faith and the manner of the Christian life are two different questions.

Spiritual Dynamics — Spiritual Life Basics addresses the second question: not what to believe, but how the Christian life is to be lived. The mechanics examined in these lessons are not abstract. They are operational: the filling of the Holy Spirit, the function of the faith-rest drill, the accumulation of metabolized doctrine in the stream of consciousness, and the problem-solving devices established on the defense line of the soul. These are the means by which the righteousness of God — revealed from faith to faith — becomes the lived experience of the believer in time.

Spiritual Dynamics – Spiritual Life Basics

Lesson One

The Portfolio of Invisible Assets — epignosis, kardia, the riches of His grace

Eph. 1:17-18 “that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give you the Spirit of wisdom and of revelation in the knowledge of him, having the eyes of your hearts enlightened, that you may know what is the hope to which he has called you, what are the riches of his glorious inheritance in the saints” (ESV)
Corrected translation: in order that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give you a lifestyle of wisdom and revelation concerning Him through metabolized doctrine, in order that the eyes of your right lobe may be enlightened so that you may know what is the confidence of His calling, what are the riches of the glory of His inheritance in the saints

This lesson inaugurates the Spiritual Dynamics series, a study of the mechanics of the spiritual life in the Church Age. The central thesis is simple but revolutionary: God has provided for every Church Age believer a unique spiritual life, complete with uniform principles and divine mandates, yet designed to operate through—not against—the believer's individual personality. The spiritual life is not personality modification; it is the consistent intake and application of Bible doctrine within the framework of grace.

I. The Spiritual Life and Personality

A persistent misunderstanding equates spirituality with a particular personality type. Legalism demands conformity to an external mold—solemn, reserved, or artificially exuberant—depending on the tradition. Role-model emulation compounds the error: believers attempt to imitate the mannerisms of those they admire rather than developing their own capacity for doctrine.

The spiritual life God has provided is uniform in its mandates, its modus operandi, and its support systems. It is not uniform in personality expression. Believers with vastly different temperaments—analytical or intuitive, reserved or gregarious, contemplative or active—all operate within the same doctrinal framework. The common element is not personality but the divine provision that sustains them: the portfolio of invisible assets.

Every Church Age believer possesses this portfolio. It was prepared in eternity past by the omniscience of God the Father and credited to the believer's account at the moment of salvation. The portfolio contains uniform categories, yet variations within those categories account for differences in spiritual gifts and operational assignments. What unites every believer is not temperament but position: all are in the hand of God, all are recipients of the same grace provision, all are called to the same process of spiritual growth.

II. The Portfolio of Invisible Assets

The term 'portfolio' is borrowed from finance and investment—a collection of assets held by an owner. In the spiritual life, the portfolio of invisible assets represents the total divine provision given to every believer at salvation. This provision is invisible because it cannot be measured by human metrics; it is not wealth in the monetary sense, nor success by worldly standards.

A. Scriptural Designations

Several passages in the Pauline epistles describe this portfolio under different titles, each emphasizing a distinct aspect of the divine provision:

Ephesians 1:6–7: 'to the praise of the glory of His grace, which He freely bestowed on us in the Beloved. In Him we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of His grace.' The prepositional phrase kata to ploutos tēs charitos autou (κατὰ τὸ πλοῦτος τῆς χάριτος αὐτοῦ) establishes the basis of the portfolio: the riches of divine grace.

Ephesians 1:18: 'the riches of the glory of His inheritance in the saints.' This phrase defines the portfolio as an inheritance—something received, not earned. The believer is an heir of God, and the inheritance includes provisions for both time and eternity.

Ephesians 3:16: 'that He would grant you, according to the riches of His glory, to be strengthened with power through His Spirit in the inner man.' Here the portfolio is connected to the Spirit's empowerment of the believer's inner life—the right lobe of the soul.

Romans 9:23: 'in order to make known the riches of His glory upon vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory.' The portfolio was prepared in eternity past for believers who are designated 'vessels of mercy.'

Philippians 4:19: 'And my God will supply all your needs according to His riches in glory in Christ Jesus.' This verse is often misapplied to mean immediate material provision. In context, it refers to the believer's access to the portfolio through the process of doctrine intake and spiritual growth.

Colossians 2:2–3: 'attaining to all the wealth of full assurance of understanding, resulting in a true knowledge of God's mystery, that is, Christ Himself.' The portfolio produces 'wealth of full assurance'—confidence in understanding God, self, and human relationships—through epignosis (ἐπίγνωσις), metabolized doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness.

B. Origin in Eternity Past

The omniscience of God the Father knew every Church Age believer before creation. There never was a time when God did not know who would believe in Christ during this dispensation. Consequently, in eternity past—before the creation of angelic beings, before the creation of mankind—God the Father prepared a portfolio of invisible assets for every believer in the Church.

This portfolio was not credited to the believer's account until the moment of salvation by faith in Christ. At that instant, the believer received everything necessary for the spiritual life: positional sanctification, the indwelling of the Trinity, spiritual gifts, and escrow blessings for time and eternity. The portfolio is complete; nothing can be added to it by human effort.

III. The Uniqueness of the Church Age

The portfolio of invisible assets is a Church Age distinctive. No other dispensation—neither the Age of the Gentiles, the Age of Israel, the dispensation of the Hypostatic Union, the Tribulation, nor the Millennium—provides every believer with this comprehensive spiritual endowment.

In previous dispensations, the pattern resembled an opera: there were leading figures—prophets, judges, kings—who received special endowments, while the majority of believers functioned as support. Elijah had an audience; David had an army; Moses had a nation. The ordinary believer in Israel did not possess the same spiritual equipment as the prophet or the king.

The Church Age reverses this pattern. Every believer—regardless of social standing, intellectual ability, or natural talent—possesses the same portfolio. There is no longer a distinction between the spiritual elite and the spiritual masses. The ordinary believer has been given an extraordinary provision, and through the consistent intake of Bible doctrine, that believer can advance to spiritual maturity and become a hero of the faith.

This is why Church Age doctrine is called 'mystery doctrine.' It was not revealed in the Old Testament; it was prophesied by Christ during the dispensation of the Hypostatic Union and became operational at Pentecost. The mystery is not something hidden from believers today—it has been revealed—but it was hidden from all previous generations.

IV. The Right Lobe and the Stream of Consciousness

A. The Heart in Scripture

The Greek noun kardia (καρδία) and the Hebrew noun lev (לֵב) are both translated 'heart' in English versions. In biblical anthropology, the heart is not the physical organ but the right lobe of the soul—the place where perception, thought, motivation, and decision-making occur.

The right lobe contains what may be called the stream of consciousness—the continuous flow of thought, perception, and volition that constitutes the believer's mental life. This stream of consciousness has multiple compartments, each corresponding to a different function of the soul: the frame of reference, memory center, vocabulary and categorical storage, norms and standards, launching pad, and the emotional complex.

B. The Eyes of the Right Lobe

Ephesians 1:18 uses a striking metaphor: 'the eyes of your kardia.' The plural 'eyes' corresponds to the multiple compartments of the stream of consciousness. Each compartment functions as a lens through which the believer perceives reality. When these compartments are filled with metabolized doctrine (epignosis), they are 'enlightened'—capable of perceiving spiritual truth and applying it to life.

The prayer of Ephesians 1:17–18 is that believers would receive a 'lifestyle of wisdom'—not sporadic insight but a consistent pattern of thinking and living governed by doctrine. This lifestyle is produced when the eyes of the right lobe are enlightened, enabling the believer to understand three things: (1) the confidence of God's calling—the believer's identity and destiny in Christ; (2) the riches of God's inheritance—the portfolio of invisible assets; and (3) the surpassing greatness of God's power toward believers—the operational provision for the spiritual life.

C. Blackout and Scar Tissue

The stream of consciousness can be polluted by two related phenomena: blackout of the soul and scar tissue of the soul. Blackout occurs when the right lobe is darkened by sustained negative volition toward doctrine. Scar tissue is the hardened residue of this negative volition—accumulated resistance to truth that progressively impairs the believer's capacity to perceive and respond to doctrine.

These conditions are not corrected instantly. The replacement of blackout and scar tissue with metabolized doctrine is a process—a gradual transformation that requires consistent exposure to Bible teaching over time. There is no shortcut, no crisis experience, no emotional decision that can substitute for the daily discipline of doctrine intake.

V. The Process of Spiritual Growth

A. Priority of Doctrine

The spiritual life cannot function apart from the accumulation of metabolized doctrine. This is not optional equipment; it is the fuel that powers the entire system. Without doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness, the believer cannot utilize the portfolio of invisible assets, cannot develop the capacity for love and happiness, and cannot fulfill the purpose for which God has called him.

The priority of doctrine must be a matter of volition. No one can force the believer to learn; no pastor can compel application. The believer must decide that Bible doctrine will occupy the number one position in his scale of values, and he must maintain that decision through the ups and downs of daily life.

B. Consistency Over Time

Spiritual growth is not measured by isolated moments of insight but by sustained progress over time. The believer who attends Bible class sporadically will never advance. The believer who listens with half attention, applying nothing to himself, will accumulate no doctrine. The believer who projects every convicting passage onto others while exempting himself will remain in a state of spiritual infancy.

The process requires what might be called 'ducks on the pond'—having one's priorities aligned and one's volition consistently directed toward doctrine. It requires showing up, paying attention, and applying what is taught. Over time, the accumulated doctrine pushes out the blackout and scar tissue, heals the damage in the subconscious, and produces the capacity for the believer to live his spiritual life.

C. No Instant Solutions

The contemporary demand for instant results has no application to the spiritual life. There is no crisis experience that produces instant maturity. There is no emotional decision that substitutes for years of doctrine intake. There is no shortcut around the process.

Believers who attempt to bypass the process—through emotional experiences, legalistic conformity, or role-model emulation—will find that they have abandoned their spiritual life without realizing it. The divine provision remains unused; the portfolio sits idle; the believer drifts into reversionism and discipline rather than advancing to maturity and blessing.

VI. The Alternative: Discipline or Glory

The believer who neglects the spiritual life does not escape consequences. Divine discipline is the inevitable result of sustained negative volition. Warning discipline comes first—the gentle knock at the door (Rev. 3:20 applied to the believer in reversionism). Intensive discipline follows if warning is ignored. And if the believer persists in obnoxiousness—the refusal to use the divine provision—the sin unto death terminates his earthly life.

The alternative is glory. The believer who stays in the process, who maintains doctrine as the first priority, who allows the Spirit to enlighten the eyes of his right lobe, advances to spiritual maturity. At maturity, the believer cracks the maturity barrier and enters supergrace—a status of blessing that exceeds anything the world can offer. Beyond supergrace lies ultra-supergrace, and beyond that, the eternal rewards deposited in the believer's escrow account in heaven.

The choice is the believer's. God has provided the portfolio. God has revealed the mystery doctrine. God has given every Church Age believer a spiritual life that no previous generation possessed. The only variable is volition: will the believer enter the process and stay with it, or will he abandon the provision and forfeit the glory?

Conclusions from Lesson One

1. The spiritual life is not personality modification. God has provided a uniform system of mandates and principles that operates through the believer's individual personality, not against it. Believers with vastly different temperaments all function within the same doctrinal framework.

2. Every Church Age believer possesses a portfolio of invisible assets. This portfolio was prepared in eternity past by the omniscience of God the Father and credited to the believer's account at the moment of salvation. It contains everything necessary for the spiritual life.

3. The portfolio is described by multiple titles in Scripture. 'The riches of His grace' (Eph. 1:7), 'the riches of the glory of His inheritance' (Eph. 1:18), 'the riches of His glory' (Eph. 3:16), and 'His riches in glory in Christ Jesus' (Phil. 4:19) all refer to the same divine provision.

4. The Church Age is unique in the scope of its provision. No other dispensation provides every believer with this comprehensive spiritual endowment. The ordinary Church Age believer possesses spiritual equipment that exceeds anything given to the masses in previous ages.

5. The right lobe of the soul is the operational center of the spiritual life. The Greek kardia and Hebrew lev refer not to the physical heart but to the stream of consciousness where perception, thought, and decision occur. The 'eyes' of the right lobe are the multiple compartments that must be enlightened by metabolized doctrine.

6. Spiritual growth is a process, not an event. There is no instant cure, no crisis experience, no emotional decision that produces maturity. The replacement of blackout and scar tissue with metabolized doctrine requires consistent exposure to Bible teaching over time.

7. Volition is the key variable. God has provided the portfolio and revealed the mystery doctrine. The believer must decide to enter the process and maintain doctrine as the first priority. The alternative to growth is discipline; the outcome of growth is glory.

Lesson Two

The Mystery of the Faith — musterion, epignosis, eusebia — The Portfolio of Invisible Assets

1 Tim. 3:9 “They must hold the mystery of the faith with a clear conscience.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: With a pure conscience, keep holding the mystery, even doctrine.

This lesson continues the introduction to Spiritual Life Basics by presenting the believer's portfolio of invisible assets — the comprehensive provision God has deposited for every Church Age believer. The focus scripture, 1 Timothy 3:9, identifies the doctrinal content of the spiritual life as 'the mystery of the faith,' a body of truth accessible only to those who are filled with the Spirit and positive toward Bible doctrine on a consistent basis.

I. The Portfolio of Invisible Assets — Overview

The origin of the believer's portfolio is God Himself — specifically, the omniscience of God. Every decision God makes is based on knowing all the facts. In contrast, human decisions are often made without full knowledge, from a position of weakness, and frequently based on false concepts of life. The portfolio God has provided for the believer is true wealth — wealth that requires no security measures, no alarm systems, no protection from theft. The security system is God's own system.

The portfolio is organized into four categories:

1.. The Spiritual Life of the Believer — including spiritual gifts, the filling of the Spirit, metabolized doctrine, and the ten problem-solving devices.

2.. Spiritual Production and Spiritual Skills — the basis for Christian service, with spiritual skills prerequisite to production skills.

3.. Escrow Blessings — greater blessings for time and eternity, deposited by God the Father as grantor, conveyed by Christ as escrow officer when the believer executes the spiritual life.

4.. Unique Assets of the Church Age — including the baptism of the Spirit, the universal priesthood, the indwelling of the Trinity, and the unique availability of divine power.

II. Category One — The Spiritual Life

A. Definition and Location

The spiritual life is the basic concept in the believer's portfolio of invisible assets. As a believer in Jesus Christ, you alone are the only one who can live your spiritual life. It was assigned to you in eternity past when God knew you would believe in Christ. To abandon it is one of the greatest tragedies in life.

The spiritual life is located in the right lobe of the soul, called the heart in Scripture, within the seven compartments of the stream of consciousness: frame of reference, memory center, vocabulary storage, categorical storage, norms and standards, the wisdom compartment, and the subconscious. This is the vault that contains all the wealth of the universe — thought. Divine thought. The thinking of Jesus Christ, called 'the mind of Christ.'

2 Cor. 4:18 “as we look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: We look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen are temporal, but the things which are not seen are eternal.

B. The Power of the Spiritual Life

The spiritual life function always operates under divine power. Two powers cannot be ignored: the power of God through the filling of the Spirit, and the power of the Word of God. As born-again believers, when we ignore these powers, we will first self-destruct through our own bad decisions, then fail to take responsibility for them, which places us in direct contact with the Supreme Court of Heaven — the justice of God.

Eph. 1:19-20 “and what is the immeasurable greatness of his power toward us who believe, according to the working of his great might that he worked in Christ when he raised him from the dead and seated him at his right hand in the heavenly places” (ESV)
Corrected translation: And what is the surpassing greatness of His power to us who have believed, in the working — the energy, the function — of His superior power, which He put into operation by means of Christ when He raised Him from the dead and enthroned Him at His own right hand in the heavens.

C. The Ten Problem-Solving Devices

Extrapolations from metabolized doctrine form the ten problem-solving devices, which constitute the flat line of the soul. These devices prevent the outside pressures of adversity from becoming the inside pressures of stress in the soul. When these defenses are breached, stress in the soul results in sin nature control of the command post — the heart — and the thought pattern is disrupted. The believer is then put out of action with regard to the spiritual life.

III. Enemies of the Spiritual Life — 1 Timothy 6:3-5

1 Tim. 6:3-5 “If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not agree with the sound words of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching that accords with godliness, he is puffed up with conceit and understands nothing. He has an unhealthy craving for controversy and for quarrels about words, which produce envy, dissension, slander, evil suspicions, and constant friction among people who are depraved in mind and deprived of the truth, imagining that godliness is a means of gain.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not concur with sound doctrine, even the doctrines pertaining to the spiritual life, he is arrogant, understanding nothing, but has a morbid obsession about controversies and verbal conflicts, from which originate jealousy, discord, evil speculations, and constant friction among those who are corrupt in mind and deprived of the truth.

A. The Word Eusebia

The Greek noun eusebia (εὐσέβεια) is commonly translated 'godliness,' but this rendering is inadequate. The word does not denote godlikeness or any system of self-righteousness designed to impress God. Rather, eusebia refers to the spiritual life itself — the basis from Bible doctrine in the stream of consciousness for having true Christian fellowship, which is fellowship with God.

B. Arrogance Defined

The passage defines arrogance in two ways. First, 'understanding nothing' — this is spiritual denial. It is the failure to understand the Christian way of life, what constitutes the spiritual life, what spiritual dynamics is, and why believers exist after salvation. Second, 'morbid obsession about controversies' — arrogance perpetuated not only denies reality but develops a false perception of reality. The morbid obsession involves never applying Scripture to oneself while applying it to everyone else.

Self-righteous arrogance says, 'That doesn't apply to me,' or 'That is wrong.' This sets controversy in the soul — an antagonistic attitude that becomes a system of human self-righteousness. None of us can resist truth without operating on human dynamics. There is no place in the Christian way of life for human dynamics.

C. The Protocol Plan of God

The Protocol Plan of God for the Church Age believer is a rigid, long-established code and procedure — long-established meaning before time began, billions of years ago — prescribing complete deference to superior power and authority. The word 'superior' indicates that believers are all on the same team but do not all play the same position. God the Holy Spirit, as the coach, assigns the positions. The authority to teach the Word is given to the spiritual gift of pastor-teacher. The spiritual life is never designed to change the personality — the issue is what is in the soul.

D. The Results of Morbid Obsession

Lesson Three

Forgetting What Lies Behind — The Forward Orientation of the Spiritual Life

Phil. 3:13-15 — Forward Momentum in the Spiritual Life

Phil. 3:13-15 “Brothers, I do not consider that I have made it my own. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus. Corrected translation: Brothers, I do not regard myself as having laid hold of it all yet. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and reaching forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the objective for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus. Let us therefore, as many as are mature, have this attitude. And if in anything you have a different attitude, God will reveal that to you.

This lesson continues the Spiritual Life Basics series by establishing a foundational orientation principle: the spiritual life demands forward momentum. Philippians 3:13-15 provides the doctrinal basis for understanding that the believer's post-salvation experience is not defined by retrospection into the past but by consistent advancement toward spiritual maturity. The passage reveals that even the apostle Paul operated under this principle—he had not yet apprehended the fullness of spiritual maturity, yet he pressed forward rather than backward. This forward orientation is not a matter of personality change or external conformity but of internal doctrinal transformation in the stream of consciousness.

I. The Two Births and the Origin of the Spiritual Life

The spiritual life exists only after the second birth. Birth one (physical birth) is the origin of human life; birth two (regeneration through faith in Christ) is the origin of eternal life and the entrance into one's personal spiritual life.

A. Birth One: The Origin of Human Life

At physical birth, God the Father creates soul life and imputes it to biological life. Simultaneously, Adam's original sin is imputed to the genetically formed sin nature transmitted through the male sperm. The result is that every human being enters the world in a state of spiritual death—separated from God and incapable of producing any merit that gains divine approbation.

B. Birth Two: The Origin of Eternal Life

At the moment of faith in Jesus Christ, God the Holy Spirit creates a human spirit, and God the Father imparts His very own life to the believer. This is regeneration—being born again. It is the entrance into the spiritual life that God the Father provided in eternity past within the believer's portfolio of invisible assets.

C. The Spiritual Life Is Post-Salvation

There is no spiritual life related to physical birth. The spiritual life exists exclusively after regeneration. However, though the spiritual life is mandated by the Word of God for every born-again believer, it remains optional in practice. The believer may choose to live the spiritual life or may choose to neglect it. The shrinking pivot of mature believers in any generation is evidence that the spiritual life mandate is rarely obeyed.

II. The Forward Orientation of the Spiritual Life

The first principle of the spiritual life is that it demands the believer look forward from the point of salvation rather than backward. Retrospection into the past—whether to explain failures, assign blame, or justify present weakness—is incompatible with living the spiritual life.

A. The Danger of Backward Retrospection

Many believers operate under the assumption that they are the way they are because of experiences in the past. This backward orientation creates a pattern of blaming others for one's own bad decisions. The result is a position of weakness rather than the spiritual strength that doctrine provides.

Backward retrospection typically manifests in one of several forms: blaming parents for childhood experiences, blaming environment for current failures, or using past injustices as justification for present dysfunction. While such experiences may be real and genuinely unjust, the spiritual life is not lived by dwelling on them.

B. The Exception: Awareness for the Purpose of Advancement

The one legitimate use of retrospection is awareness of garbage in the subconscious and in the stream of consciousness. Such awareness is not for the purpose of blame but for the purpose of identifying what must be dealt with through Bible doctrine. Awareness should produce a renewed desire for doctrine and the answers the Word of God provides—not a fixation on the past.

C. The Pauline Model

In Philippians 3:13, Paul states that he does not regard himself as having laid hold of it all yet—that is, he has not yet apprehended the fullness of the spiritual life and spiritual maturity. Yet his response is not retrospection but forward momentum: forgetting what lies behind and reaching forward to what lies ahead. The verb epekteinomenos (ἐπεκτεινόμενος) conveys the image of stretching forward with intensity, as a runner strains toward the finish line.

III. The Spiritual Life Does Not Change Personality

A common misconception is that the spiritual life requires a change in personality. This is not the case. The spiritual life is concerned with what occurs in the stream of consciousness—thinking, motivation, decision—not with external conformity to some mold of legalism or self-righteous expectations.

A. Personality Formation

Personality is formed through a combination of influences: biological life in the womb, the presence of the sin nature in the cell structure of the body, environment after birth, and the imputations that occur at birth (soul life and Adam's original sin). These factors combine to produce a unique personality in every human being. No two individuals are identical.

B. The Spiritual Life Operates Within Personality

At the point of regeneration, God gives each believer his very own spiritual life. This spiritual life never includes a mandate to change personality. The spiritual life, when it begins to take hold in the stream of consciousness, produces internal changes—changes in thinking, motivation, and values—but not external personality conformity.

The believer who is happy by temperament may remain happy. The believer who is reserved by temperament may remain reserved. The spiritual life does not demand that one personality type become another. What the spiritual life demands is doctrinal content in the soul, not personality modification.

C. The Error of Legalistic Manipulation

Legalism attempts to force personality conformity through guilt manipulation, role-model imposition, and external pressure. This approach substitutes self-righteous arrogance and the energy of the flesh for grace orientation and doctrinal orientation. The result is not spirituality but counterfeit spirituality—a life of reaction to external pressure rather than response to internal doctrine.

The spiritual life has in common with all believers a uniform system of mandates and problem-solving devices. These are represented by doctrine in the soul, not by external conformity to human standards. The believer's task is to acquire doctrine, not to become a clone of someone else's personality.

IV. Authority Orientation and the Spiritual Life

Parents represent the first authority in human life and the basic authority of childhood. Reaction to parental authority often becomes the template for reaction to all other categories of authority throughout life.

A. The Pattern of Authority Reaction

Many believers do not understand why they react to certain situations or make certain decisions. The explanation often lies in unresolved reaction to parental authority that has been suppressed through denial and dissociation. This garbage in the subconscious creates automatic patterns of resentment, rejection, denial, and projection whenever authority is encountered—whether in marriage, employment, government, or the local church.

B. The Problem of Unforgiveness

Some believers speak of forgiving their parents, yet the test of genuine forgiveness is the absence of postmortem bitterness. When one claims to forgive but subsequently enters into the sins of the arrogance complex—jealousy, bitterness, vindictiveness, implacability, hatred, self-pity, malice, revenge—the forgiveness is merely verbal, not operational. True forgiveness, functioning under the spiritual life, forgives and forgets. The person or injustice ceases to be an issue in the soul, and there is no planning or thinking about it.

C. The Doctrinal Solution

Birth separates the individual from parents so that parental failures and sin nature functions can be handled after regeneration—not immediately, because the spiritual life is a process, but progressively through the daily intake of Bible doctrine. The genes combined in conception are never an excuse for failure. Childhood environment is never an excuse for failure. As a born-again believer, God has provided a superimposing and dynamic power far greater than any influence of the past.

V. The Mechanics of Forward Momentum

Philippians 3:13-15 provides the mechanics for forward momentum in the spiritual life. These mechanics are not instant or automatic but require consistent application over time.

A. Forgetting What Lies Behind

The phrase forgetting what lies behind does not mean amnesia or pretending the past did not occur. It means that the past no longer controls the present. The believer who is living the spiritual life does not allow past failures, past injustices, or past environment to determine present decisions and attitudes.

This forgetting is not accomplished by willpower or psychological technique. It is accomplished through the cognition, inculcation, and metabolization of Bible doctrine in the stream of consciousness. On a continual basis, this process destroys the garbage in the subconscious while simultaneously producing forward momentum toward spiritual maturity.

B. Pressing Toward the Objective

Paul states, I press on toward the objective for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus. The verb diōkō (διώκω) means to pursue with intensity, to press forward with determination. The objective is the high ground of spiritual maturity. The prize is the upward call—the glorification of God through a life lived in conformity to the spiritual life He has provided.

C. The Attitude of Maturity

Paul concludes in verse 15: Let us therefore, as many as are mature, have this attitude. The adjective teleios (τέλειος) refers to those who have reached spiritual maturity—those who have cracked the maturity barrier through sustained intake of Bible doctrine. The attitude of maturity is forward orientation, not backward retrospection.

Paul adds a significant qualifier: if anyone has a different attitude, God will reveal that to them. The primary means of this revelation is the teaching of the Word of God. The believer who sits under consistent doctrinal instruction will have wrong attitudes exposed and corrected through the ministry of the Holy Spirit applying doctrine to the soul.

VI. Principles of Spiritual Advancement

Several principles emerge from this study that govern advancement in the spiritual life.

A. He Who Does Not Advance Goes Backward

There is no neutral position in the spiritual life. The believer is either advancing toward maturity or regressing toward reversionism. The failure to move forward is itself a form of moving backward. Stagnation is not an option.

B. He Conquers Who Endures

Success in the spiritual life comes through patient perseverance, not instant experience. The spiritual life is a day-by-day process, not a one-time crisis event. The believer who endures in consistent doctrinal intake will ultimately conquer the obstacles to spiritual maturity.

C. Doctrine Conquers All

Bible doctrine metabolized in the soul is the conquering force in the spiritual life. Doctrine destroys the garbage of the past. Doctrine neutralizes the pressures of the present. Doctrine provides capacity for the blessings of the future. There is no substitute for doctrine.

D. Consistency with Oneself

The believer who begins with Bible doctrine must continue with Bible doctrine and become consistent in living the spiritual life. This is not a matter of conformity to external standards but of internal consistency—the same doctrinal principles operating in the soul day after day, year after year.

E. The Price of Knowledge

Every believer desires to be informed. Few are willing to pay the price. The price is consistent attendance at Bible class, consistent intake of doctrine, consistent application of what is learned. The spiritual life is not for those who want shortcuts. It is for those who are willing to invest the time and discipline required for spiritual growth.

Conclusions from Lesson Three

1. The spiritual life exists only after regeneration. Birth one is the origin of human life; birth two is the origin of eternal life and the entrance into the spiritual life provided by God in the believer's portfolio of invisible assets.

2. The spiritual life demands forward orientation, not backward retrospection. Looking backward to explain failures, assign blame, or justify weakness is incompatible with living the spiritual life. The believer must forget what lies behind and press forward toward the objective of spiritual maturity.

3. The spiritual life does not require personality change. God gives each believer a spiritual life that operates within that believer's unique personality. The mandate is doctrinal content in the soul, not external conformity to legalistic expectations.

4. Reaction to parental authority often creates patterns of reaction to all authority. Unresolved issues from childhood, suppressed through denial and dissociation, produce automatic patterns of resentment and rejection that hinder spiritual growth. The solution is not psychological technique but doctrinal intake.

5. True forgiveness is operational, not merely verbal. The test of genuine forgiveness is the absence of postmortem bitterness. The believer functioning under the spiritual life forgives, forgets, and moves on without further reaction.

6. Forward momentum is achieved through consistent doctrinal intake. Forgetting what lies behind is not accomplished by willpower but through the cognition, inculcation, and metabolization of Bible doctrine in the stream of consciousness over time.

7. The attitude of spiritual maturity is forward orientation. Those who are mature think this way—pressing toward the objective, not dwelling on the past. If a believer has a different attitude, God will reveal it through the teaching of His Word.

8. He who does not advance goes backward. There is no neutral position in the spiritual life. Stagnation is regression. The believer must maintain forward momentum through consistent doctrinal intake and application.

9. Doctrine conquers all. Bible doctrine metabolized in the soul is the conquering force that destroys the garbage of the past, neutralizes present pressures, and provides capacity for future blessings.

10. The price of spiritual knowledge is consistent discipline. Every believer desires to be informed, but few are willing to pay the price of consistent attendance, intake, and application. The spiritual life rewards those who invest the required discipline.

Ephesians 4:30-32 — The relationship between morality and the spiritual life — Divine establishment principles — The believer's higher calling — Three dangers: morality as salvation, sin that destroys fellowship, legalistic arrogance — λυπέω (lupeō), σφραγίζω (sphragizō), χαρίζομαι (charizomai)

This lesson addresses the critical distinction between morality and the spiritual life. The believer is mandated to be moral, but he is also mandated to function in a realm far higher than the establishment morality available to all mankind. Morality pertains to divine establishment—right conduct within the framework of volition, marriage, family, and nationalism. The spiritual life, by contrast, is the exclusive possession of the regenerate believer, a dimension of existence that operates under the filling of the Holy Spirit and the metabolized doctrine circulating in the soul. Understanding this distinction protects the believer from three dangers: substituting morality for salvation, allowing immorality to destroy fellowship, and permitting legalistic arrogance to corrupt the spiritual life from within.

I. Morality Defined

Morality is right conduct and excellence in the practice and function of the laws of divine establishment. It was first introduced to the world through Codex Number Three of the Mosaic Law and has since been amplified in the New Testament, with a particularly strong passage in Romans 13:1-10. Morality is conformity to the rules of right conduct as delineated in establishment principles—virtue in sexual matters, right conduct in marriage, proper function in the family, and conformity to the laws of a national entity.

Divine institutions are not for believers alone but for all mankind, believer and unbeliever alike. Consequently, morality belongs to the entire human race. It is the heritage of those who are born once and those who are born twice. Both categories must recognize that morality is the only way the human race can coexist—maintaining privacy, freedom, and protection from enemies both without and within the national entity.

The Word of God establishes the importance of the military in protecting freedom through victory, and it delineates a system of laws designed to protect citizens from criminals—enemies of the nation from within. When a criminal has been proven guilty under a system of jurisprudence, he has forfeited the rights that morality was designed to protect. The believer is mandated by the Word of God to be a moral person and to fulfill his obligation of right conduct within the framework of the national entity, as our Lord Himself emphasized: render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar's and to God the things that are God's.

II. The Believer's Higher Calling

The born-again believer faces a dual mandate from the Word of God. On the one hand, he is mandated to be moral. On the other hand, he is mandated to live in a realm far greater and higher than the system of morality found in establishment. He is mandated to a higher responsibility and a higher purpose—a life that deals not only with the mankind with whom he associates but also with the billions of angels who observe the born-again believer. The prehistoric angelic conflict is being resolved especially in the Church Age by believers who make Bible doctrine their number one priority and whose spiritual values recognize the invisible impact that any believer, regardless of station in life, can have in human history.

The principle is simple: anything that the unbeliever can do is not the spiritual life. The unbeliever can be highly moral and produce excellent conduct, but the spiritual life is the monopoly of the believer. Morality belongs to the entire human race; the spiritual life is given to every believer at salvation as part of his portfolio of invisible assets. This does not mean morality is ever rejected; rather, there is a higher calling for every member of the royal family of God in this dispensation.

III. Three Dangers to the Spiritual Life

A. First Danger: Morality as the Way of Salvation

Morality is a lifestyle, but it is not the way of salvation. When morality is adopted as the way of salvation, there is no salvation, for only faith in Jesus Christ can provide eternal life.

Gal. 2:16 “Yet we know that a person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ, so we also have believed in Christ Jesus, in order to be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law, because by works of the law no one will be justified.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: Knowing that a person is not justified by the works of the law but through faith, even we have believed in Christ Jesus that we might be justified by faith in Christ Jesus and not by the works of the law, for by the works of the law no flesh will be justified.

Israel's tragedy was keeping Codex Number Three of the Mosaic Law along with the Decalogue and extrapolating from it works and good deeds, then attempting to gain the approbation of God on the basis of those works. But salvation is by faith in Jesus Christ, and by the law is only the knowledge of sin—the understanding that those sins were judged by Christ on the cross.

B. Second Danger: Immorality That Destroys Fellowship

Many personal sins also violate the laws of divine establishment as they relate to morality. Since the power of the spiritual life is the filling of the Holy Spirit, and since any form of sin removes that power and control, the believer loses fellowship with God. Violation of morality principles related to sin is therefore destructive to the spiritual life unless corrected by rebound.

When the believer violates the morality principles of the Mosaic Law, he is in a state of sin which destroys his spiritual life. Sexual sins, criminal sins, chemical sins such as drug abuse and drunkenness—all result in sin nature control of the soul, antinomianism, reversionism, and ultimately moral or immoral degeneracy.

At the moment of faith in Jesus Christ, the Holy Spirit enters the believer into union with Christ—a position that can never be lost. But the believer also enters the operational divine dynasphere, the place of the filling of the Spirit. When he sins, he is outside that operational sphere. The only way to recover is to use the privacy of his priesthood: naming sins privately to God. According to 1 John 1:9, if we acknowledge our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

The Greek verb homologeō (ὁμολογέω) is a legal term meaning to cite precedents, to prove one's case, or to confess guilt. It was used in this legal sense in fifth-century Athens. We as believers sin and fail, and when we do, we are out of fellowship. We cite, name, and acknowledge in the privacy of our priesthood those sins to God. Those sins went to the cross and were judged; therefore He is faithful and justified in forgiving us and cleansing us from all unrighteousness. While morality is not the spiritual life as such, lack of morality destroys the spiritual life as such.

C. Third Danger: Legalistic Arrogance

Just as salvation is by grace through faith in Christ, so the spiritual life is based on post-salvation grace from God. God provides spiritual power to execute and fulfill His plan for our lives, and this power exists in two categories: the power of the filling of the Spirit and the power of metabolized doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness.

The third danger relates to legalism. Morality and the works of the Mosaic Law have a way of squeezing out grace, leaving a residue of arrogance and the sins of the soul. Morality without humility produces a subtle form of arrogance destructive to the spiritual life.

God has provided metabolized Bible doctrine which, when placed on the defense line of the soul, functions as an instant reaction force. This prevents outside pressures of adversity, prosperity, denial, and projection from becoming inside pressures of stress in the soul. Stress in the soul is tantamount to sin nature control, which moves the believer toward the sins of the soul: the arrogance complex and the emotional complex. The sin nature has two trends: one toward self-righteousness and legalism, the other toward antinomianism. Both lead through the stages of reversionism, ending in Christian moral degeneracy or Christian immoral degeneracy. Both categories of believers are losers in time, though they do not lose their salvation.

God makes war against the arrogant believer but gives grace to the humble believer (James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5). The spiritual life can only function under grace orientation and doctrinal orientation—metabolized doctrine actually circulating in the stream of consciousness. When the believer becomes proud of his morality, uses his morality as leverage to manipulate others, or enters into civil disobedience, he has destroyed his spiritual life through violation of the morality code of the Word of God. Civil disobedience violates Romans 13:1-10. Activism adopts the anti-morality concept that the end justifies the means, destroying property, abolishing privacy, and violating all principles of freedom. Believers involved in such activities have entered the status of grieving and quenching the Holy Spirit.

When the sin nature takes control of the moral person, the sins of the arrogance complex take complete control: jealousy, bitterness, vindictiveness, implacability, hatred, vituperation, vilification, malice, revenge motivation, and revenge operation. Simultaneously, the sins of the emotional complex surface: fear, worry, anxiety, panic, irrationality, dissociation, denial, projection, animosity, loathing, self-pity, and above all the destructive guilt complex. The sins of the lust pattern then become pseudo-motivation for life: power lust, approbation lust, social and sexual lust, pleasure and chemical lust, monetary lust, criminal lust, crusader lust, and revenge lust.

IV. Forgiveness as an Illustration

The morality of forgiveness has problems, for it is often used as leverage—causing manipulation by guilt or even justifying wrong conduct. The function of forgiveness in the spiritual life avoids these distractions. The warning to the spiritual life regarding forgiveness is given in Ephesians 4:30-32.

The passage begins with the present active imperative of lupeō (λυπέω): 'Stop grieving the Holy Spirit.' The present imperative with a negative means to stop an action already in progress. Born-again believers in Ephesus—where the first great pivot of the Church Age was formed, the pivot that would transform the Roman Empire during the period of the Antonine Caesars (96–192 AD)—were grieving the Spirit. They had morality but were not using their spiritual life.

The verb sphragizō (σφραγίζω) refers to the sealing of the Spirit—a signature guarantee. This seal guarantees the Spirit's ministry in efficacious grace, regeneration, eternal life, eternal security, and the fact that each believer possesses his very own spiritual life which only he can live. At the moment of faith in Christ, God the Holy Spirit creates a human spirit, and God the Father imputes eternal life to that human spirit. The believer becomes trichotomous, possessing something far greater than the immortality of the soul—the very life of God.

Having established that these believers were suffering from a setback to their spiritual life, the passage provides the illustration pertinent to them: 'All bitterness'—one of the sins of the arrogance complex—'and wrath'—from the emotional complex—'and quarreling and slander'—back to the arrogance complex. These are the ingredients of false forgiveness. They must be removed because they are distractions from living the spiritual life. 'Along with malice'—the lust to inflict suffering and injury on the offender even after saying 'I forgive you.'

Verse 32 presents what is required in the spiritual life, placing it on a higher plane than any other factor in life: 'Become kind toward one another'—grace orientation is one of the battalions on the defense line of the soul. 'Compassionate'—impersonal love is another battalion. 'Forgiving each other just as God also by means of Christ has forgiven us.' The verb charizomai (χαρίζομαι) means to give graciously, to forgive freely. How did God forgive us? He blotted out our transgressions at the moment we believed in Christ. Every transgression was blotted out. This is spiritual forgetting—forgiveness without reaction, forgiveness not accompanied by the sins of the soul.

Reaction leads to denial, denial leads to projection, and the projection is bitterness after saying 'I forgive you.' That means you are outside of your spiritual life, grieving the Holy Spirit. When you have any bitterness toward anyone who has wronged you—whether justly or unjustly—the issue is your reaction. If you say 'I forgive' and then you are bitter, vindictive, full of malice, or entering into vituperation, then as a born-again believer you are not living the spiritual life. You may even violate the laws of divine establishment and morality at the same time by taking the matter to civil court—now you are not only grieving the Spirit but also acting immorally.

The spiritual life places personal differences in the hands of the Supreme Court of Heaven, where the believer knows he will receive perfect justice. God's magnificent wisdom and justice deal with the problem while the believer stays out of it and keeps advancing in the spiritual life. This is a magnificent virtue rarely practiced, but understanding its basis in doctrine makes its execution possible.

V. The Place of Morality in the Protocol Plan of God

Morality is the doctrine of the duties and responsibilities of all mankind—believer and unbeliever alike—in the direction of establishment. It exists to perpetuate the human race in the post-diluvian civilization from Noah to the Second Advent and to fulfill the divine purpose in the creation of mankind. Morality was designed for compatibility with establishment principles.

While the spiritual life is for believers only, this does not imply that the spiritual life rejects morality. As a dimension related to regeneration, moral commands are directed to believers even though the subject matter is establishment.

Eph. 5:3 “But sexual immorality and all impurity or covetousness must not even be named among you, as is proper among saints.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: Fornication and all licentiousness or insatiable erotic desire should not even be mentioned by you, as is protocol for the saints.

The phrase 'as is protocol for the saints' reveals the name of God's plan: the Protocol Plan of God. This verse forbids three categories of sexual sins to believers: normal sexual sins (adultery, fornication, promiscuity), abnormal sexual sins (homosexuality, lesbianism, bestiality, necrophilia, voyeurism), and criminal sexual sins (abuse of children, pimping, pandering, prostitution, sexual mutilation). Throughout human history, believers have at some time, someplace, practiced all of these things—which emphasizes that morality is a part of the spiritual life, not in contradiction to it, but never to be allowed to produce arrogance.

VI. Limitations of Morality

Despite its importance in establishment, morality has definite limitations within the Protocol Plan of God. Four limitations must be understood:

1.. Morality cannot provide eternal salvation. 'Knowing that a person is not justified by the works of the law but through faith' (Gal. 2:16).

2.. Morality cannot produce the spiritual life. 'Did you receive the Holy Spirit by the works of the law or by the hearing of faith?' (Gal. 3:2).

3.. Morality cannot advance the believer in the Protocol Plan of God. 'Are you so foolish, having begun by means of the Holy Spirit, that you are now trying to be perfected by the flesh?' (Gal. 3:3).

4.. Immorality can hinder the believer from executing the plan of God. Immorality puts the believer out of fellowship, removing the power necessary for spiritual advance.

Morality is designed for the function of freedom, making evangelism possible in every generation of human history. It is designed for client nation function and for the freedom necessary to resolve the prehistoric angelic conflict. Morality fulfills the divine institutions of volition, marriage, family, and nationalism. Understanding its proper place ensures that the believer neither ignores morality nor justifies any activity classified in theology as antinomianism.

7.. Grieving the Holy Spirit: The command to stop grieving the Spirit (Eph. 4:30) indicates that believers in Ephesus had morality but were not using their spiritual life.

8.. The sealing of the Spirit: The sealing ministry of the Spirit is a signature guarantee of efficacious grace, regeneration, eternal life, eternal security, and the believer's possession of his very own spiritual life.

10.. True forgiveness: Spiritual forgiveness means forgiving as God in Christ forgave us—forgiving and forgetting, without reaction, without the sins of the soul.

11.. Supreme Court of Heaven: The spiritual life places personal differences in the hands of the Supreme Court of Heaven, where perfect justice is administered while the believer keeps advancing.

12.. Morality in the Protocol Plan: Morality is part of the spiritual life, not in contradiction to it, but it must never be allowed to produce arrogance or to substitute for grace orientation.

13.. Limitations of morality: Morality cannot provide salvation, cannot produce the spiritual life, cannot advance the believer; but immorality can hinder the believer by putting him out of fellowship.

14.. Divine design of morality: Morality is designed for freedom, making evangelism possible, sustaining client nation function, and providing the environment in which the prehistoric angelic conflict is resolved.

Lesson Five

New Wine in Old Wineskins — The Pharisees' Denial and Projection; The Call of Sinners to Salvation

Luke 5:31-32 “And Jesus answered them, 'Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. I have not come to call the righteous but sinners to repentance.'” (ESV)
Corrected translation: And Jesus answered them, 'Those who are healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. I have not come to call the self-righteous, but sinners — resulting in a change of mind.'

This lesson examines the confrontation between Jesus Christ and the Pharisees recorded in Luke 5:17-32. The passage establishes critical principles for understanding the spiritual life: the danger of ritual without reality, the mechanics of spiritual denial and projection, and the call of God that results in a change of mind about Jesus Christ. The Pharisees' self-righteous arrogance prevented them from recognizing their Messiah, while tax collectors and sinners responded to the gospel with faith.

I. The Three Codices of the Mosaic Law

The Mosaic Law is divided into three distinct codices, each serving a specific function within the divine plan for Israel. Understanding these divisions clarifies the Pharisees' fundamental error in their approach to God.

A. Codex One: The Decalogue

The first codex comprises the Ten Commandments, which establish a complete system of freedom. The Decalogue addresses sins that are antagonistic toward freedom — sins that violate the divine institutions and destroy the social fabric necessary for human flourishing. This codex is not a complete hamartiology but identifies those transgressions most destructive to ordered liberty.

B. Codex Two: Soteriology and Christology

The second codex presents a complete soteriology and Christology through the Levitical priesthood and its ritual. This codex reveals Jesus Christ as eternal God and true humanity in one person forever. The sacrifices, offerings, and ceremonies of the Levitical system illustrate in graphic detail the salvation work of Christ on the cross — His reception of the imputation of all human sins and His judgment as the substitute for mankind.

The central confession of Israel, the Shema, encapsulates this truth: Shema Yisrael, Adonai Eloheinu, Adonai Echad — 'Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one.' The sacred tetragrammaton (YHWH) was never pronounced by the Jews; they substituted Adonai (Lord). This confession declares that Jesus Christ is Israel's God, and that He is unique — the God-man, undiminished deity and true humanity in one person forever, the only Savior.

C. Codex Three: Divine Establishment and Morality

The third codex establishes a complete system of morality and the laws of divine establishment. It addresses volition (the responsibility of the individual for his own decisions), marriage, family, and government. This codex provided Israel with the framework for national blessing under the theocratic kingdom.

II. The Pharisees' Fundamental Error: Ritual Without Reality

The Pharisees made a fatal error in their approach to the Mosaic Law. They extrapolated from the Decalogue a system of self-righteousness, believing they could gain God's approbation through moral perfection. From Codex Three they amplified minute details into an elaborate system of works-righteousness. And while they continued to perform the rituals of Codex Two, speaking the words of the Shema every Sabbath, it was ritual without reality — for they had rejected Jesus Christ as Savior.

Galatians 2:16 “yet we know that a person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ, so we also have believed in Christ Jesus, in order to be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law, because by works of the law no one will be justified.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: knowing that a person is not justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ, even we have believed in Christ that we might be justified by faith in Christ and not by the works of the law. For by the works of the law no flesh will be justified.

Our Lord condemned the Pharisees repeatedly for their hypocrisy. In Matthew 23:24, He called them 'blind guides who strain out a gnat and swallow a camel.' In Matthew 23:27, He described them as 'whitewashed tombstones' — beautiful on the outside but filled with dead men's bones on the inside. This imagery captures their spiritual death: impressive external morality concealing the reality of separation from God.

III. Human Birth and Spiritual Death

The reference to 'dead men's bones' introduces a critical soteriological principle. Mortal man in copulation and conception can create biological life, which begins at the zygote stage. That single cell contains not only the genetic code for physical life but also the genetically formed sin nature, transmitted through the male because Adam deliberately sinned while Eve was deceived.

At physical birth, biological life emerges from the womb as an 'empty house.' God creates the human soul — comprising self-consciousness, volition, mentality, emotion, and conscience — and imputes it to biological life, producing a human being. Simultaneously, Adam's original sin is imputed to the genetically formed sin nature. Therefore, physical birth produces human life plus spiritual death — separation from God and the incapacity to do anything to gain divine approbation.

This is the distinction between religion and Christianity. In religion, man by his own effort seeks to gain the approbation of God. In Christianity, God seeks man through the salvation work of Jesus Christ on the cross. Birth one provides human life and spiritual death; a second birth is required.

IV. The Mechanics of Spiritual Denial and Projection

The Pharisees' condition illustrates the mechanics of spiritual denial and projection — phenomena that also affect born-again believers who fail to apply Bible doctrine to themselves.

A. Repression

The believer becomes unable to remember or be cognitively aware of feelings, thoughts, or experiences related to the garbage in his soul. The right lobe of the soul, called in Greek the kardia and in Hebrew the lev (the 'heart'), has seven compartments in which Bible doctrine should circulate. When garbage accumulates in the subconscious — reality that cannot be accepted — repression prevents conscious acknowledgment of the problem.

B. Spiritual Denial

The believer becomes divorced from reality and enters into dissociation. The New Testament describes this condition with the term dipsuchos (δίψυχος) — the 'double-souled' or unstable believer. Second Timothy 3:5 describes those who hold to an outward form of the spiritual life (morphōsis, μόρφωσις) while denying its power — the actual spiritual life itself, which is eusebeia (ευσέβεια).

2 Timothy 3:5, 7 “having the appearance of godliness, but denying its power. ... always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: holding to an outward form of the spiritual life but having denied its power ... always learning but never able to come to the epignosis knowledge of the truth.

C. Projection

When the believer has divorced himself from reality through scar tissue and blackout of the soul, projection inevitably follows. The believer takes his own flaws, failures, unacknowledged sins, lusts, and antagonisms and assigns them to others. This explains why believers gossip, malign, judge, and engage in vituperation — they are projecting the garbage from their own subconscious onto others rather than applying doctrine to themselves.

The believer who never applies doctrine to himself, instead of being challenged regarding failures in the privacy of his own priesthood, says, 'It's too bad so-and-so isn't here to hear this.' He assigns his flaws to others in every interaction. This is why some believers claim, 'I went to Bible class for a long time and doctrine doesn't work,' or 'I listen to doctrine but never seem to get anything from it.'

V. The Pharisees' Confrontation with Christ: The Healing of the Paralytic

Luke 5:17 “On one of those days, as he was teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there, who had come from every village of Galilee and Judea and from Jerusalem. And the power of the Lord was with him to heal.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: And it came to pass on one of those days, while He was teaching — and the Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there, having come from every village of Galilee, Judea, and Jerusalem — and the power of the Lord was present to heal.

The Greek employs an imperfect periphrastic construction indicating continuous action: Jesus was continually teaching. The Pharisees and teachers of the law (scribes) had gathered from throughout the land — not to learn but to criticize. They came to find fault rather than to give the teaching an objective hearing.

The miracle that follows was specifically designed to challenge the Pharisees to recognize Jesus as Messiah. Miracles in the biblical record are never primarily designed to alleviate suffering — otherwise Christ and the apostles would have been remiss, for they did not heal in every case. Rather, miracles establish the credentials of the messenger and authenticate his message.

The Paralytic's Faith and Forgiveness

A paralytic, having already heard the gospel from our Lord and believed, persuaded his friends to carry him to the house where Jesus was teaching. Unable to enter due to the crowd, they lowered him through the roof on a stretcher. Jesus, knowing the man had already believed but did not understand that his sins had been forgiven at the moment of faith, declared, 'Your sins are forgiven' (Luke 5:20).

This was precisely what the Pharisees wanted to hear. Now they could accuse Him of blasphemy: 'Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God alone?' (Luke 5:21). Their theology was correct — only God can forgive sins — but they failed to recognize that Jesus Christ is eternal God, co-equal with the Father and the Holy Spirit.

Luke 5:22-24 “When Jesus perceived their thoughts, he answered them, 'Why do you question in your hearts? Which is easier, to say, "Your sins are forgiven you," or to say, "Rise and walk"? But that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins' — he said to the man who was paralyzed — 'I say to you, rise, pick up your bed and go home.'” (ESV)
Corrected translation: When Jesus knew their thinking, He answered and said to them, 'Why are you thinking these things in your hearts? Which is easier — to say, "Your sins are forgiven," or to say, "Get up and walk"? But that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins' — He said to the paralytic — 'I say to you, get up, take your stretcher, and go home.'

Jesus, as God, knew their thoughts (omniscience). He challenged them: if He could do the visible (heal the paralytic), would they believe He could do the invisible (forgive sins)? The man immediately stood, took his stretcher, and went home praising God. The crowd was amazed, but the Pharisees remained in denial.

VI. The Conversion of Matthew and the Second Pharisaical Attack

Luke 5:27-28 “After this he went out and saw a tax collector named Levi, sitting at the tax booth. And he said to him, 'Follow me.' And leaving everything, he rose and followed him.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: After this He went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax booth. And He said to him, 'Follow me.' And leaving everything, he got up and followed Him.

Matthew (also called Levi) was a Jewish tax collector — a publican who collected taxes for the Roman government. Tax collectors were despised by their countrymen, ostracized from respectable Jewish society, and often guilty of graft. Yet Matthew had already heard the gospel from Jesus and believed. The command 'Follow me' was a post-salvation mandate related to discipleship, not salvation.

To celebrate his entrance into discipleship, Matthew threw a great banquet at his home. His guests included fellow tax collectors and 'sinners' — a Greek term (hamartōloi) that in this context refers to prostitutes and others outside respectable society. Matthew wanted to share the gospel with his friends and associates.

The Pharisees' Complaint: Denial and Projection

Luke 5:30 “And the Pharisees and their scribes grumbled at his disciples, saying, 'Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?'” (ESV)
Corrected translation: Then the Pharisees and their scribes began complaining to His disciples, saying, 'Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?'

The Greek verb gogguzō (γογγύζω) is an onomatopoetic word that 'reeks of antagonism' — it describes grumbling and complaining. The imperfect tense is inceptive: they began to complain. Unable to confront Jesus directly at this point, they approached His disciples. The Pharisees, in denial and projection, criticized Jesus for eating and drinking (the Greek pinō refers to alcoholic beverage — this was a party) with social outcasts.

VII. Jesus' Answer: The Physician and the Call of Sinners

Luke 5:31-32 “And Jesus answered them, 'Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. I have not come to call the righteous but sinners to repentance.'” (ESV)
Corrected translation: And Jesus answered them, 'Those who are healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. I have not come to call self-righteous persons but sinners — resulting in a change of mind.'

Jesus used a medical analogy: healthy people do not need a doctor; sick people do. The Pharisees considered themselves spiritually healthy — righteous through their own works. But their self-righteousness was actually spiritual sickness unto death. Jesus came to call sinners — those who recognize their spiritual bankruptcy — not the 'righteous' who trust in their own merit.

The Meaning of Metanoia

The Greek phrase eis metanoian (εἰς μετάνοιαν) employs the accusative of result: 'resulting in a change of mind.' The noun metanoia does not mean 'feeling sorry for sins' — that would be metamelomai (μεταμέλομαι). Rather, metanoia means a complete change of mind — specifically, a change of mind about Jesus Christ.

This is the mechanics of the call of God. The gospel is spiritual phenomena. The unbeliever is in spiritual brain death and cannot comprehend spiritual truth. God the Holy Spirit makes the accurate content of the gospel understandable (pneumatikos — spiritual phenomena now comprehended). This understanding goes to the left lobe as academic knowledge. At this point the spiritually dead person, with his own volition, understands the issue: believe in Christ for eternal life, or reject Christ for eternal condemnation. This is the call of God.

The response to this call is faith — the only non-meritorious system of perception. Faith has no merit in itself; all merit belongs to the object of faith, Jesus Christ. This faith accompanies (or immediately precedes) the change of mind about Christ. Sin is not the issue — Christ bore the sins of the world on the cross. Christ is the issue.

John 3:36 “Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: He who believes in the Son has eternal life; he who does not believe shall not see life, but the wrath of God abides on him.

New Spiritual Species A new creation in Christ — all racial distinctions eliminated in Christ.
Equal Privilege and Opportunity Every believer has equal access to the execution of God's plan.

The believer's position in Christ is secure — nothing can cancel these forty provisions. The believer can never 'get out' of union with Christ. However, the believer's experience — his daily function in the spiritual life — requires the filling of the Holy Spirit and the application of Bible doctrine.

God the Father in eternity past provided for each Church Age believer a portfolio of invisible assets, including the divine dynasphere — the operational palace for the spiritual life. This palace has eight gates:

The rebound technique (naming known sins to God, 1 John 1:9) restores fellowship and returns the believer to the operational sphere of the spiritual life. This is a function of the privacy of the royal priesthood — the first problem-solving device on the flat line of the soul.

Conclusions from Lesson Five

1.. The Mosaic Law's three codices. Codex One (Decalogue) establishes freedom; Codex Two (Levitical system) presents soteriology and Christology; Codex Three establishes divine establishment and morality. The Pharisees distorted all three into a system of works-righteousness.

9.. At salvation, God accomplishes forty irreversible provisions. These include regeneration, imputation of righteousness, union with Christ, royal priesthood, and the creation of a new spiritual species with equal privilege and opportunity in the plan of God.

10.. The divine dynasphere is the believer's operational palace. Through its eight gates the believer advances from the filling of the Spirit through spiritual growth to spiritual maturity. Sin removes the believer from this sphere; rebound restores him.

I. The Context — Fasting and the Pharisees' Challenge

The Pharisees' question in verse 33 constitutes the second instance of denial and projection in this passage. Having already criticized our Lord for eating with publicans and sinners, they now challenge Him regarding fasting: 'The disciples of John fast often and offer prayers, and so do the disciples of the Pharisees, but Yours eat and drink.'

A. Fasting in Jewish Practice

Fasting was a Jewish custom but never a part of the Torah or the Mosaic Law as given by Moses. It developed later as an addition to biblical practice. Fasting was ritual without reality — a reaction to national calamity and often associated with assumed humility in hopes that God would deliver the people. This was especially true during times of failure in Israel.

Fasting appears for the first time in Judges 20:26 and occurs in various historical contexts: 1 Samuel 7:6, 2 Chronicles 20:3, Joel 1:14 and 2:12, Jeremiah 36:9, Ezra 8:21, and Nehemiah 1:4. Generally, fasting was connected with prayer, apparently based on the view that prayer was more effective when offered in hunger rather than after eating.

B. Fasting in the Early Church

The pre-canon church fasted in connection with prayer in only two recorded cases: the prayer of dedication of Barnabas and Saul in Acts 13:3, and the dedication prayer for the appointment of elders at Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch in Acts 14:23. However, fasting has no spiritual connotation for the Church Age as far as the Word of God is concerned.

Fasting is not a part of the spiritual life that was provided for the believer in eternity past as part of the portfolio of invisible assets. It is obligatory only where no emphasis on the true spiritual life exists. Rejection of what the Bible teaches about the spiritual life often results in superficial overt activities such as fasting. The purpose seems to be to impress God, but God is impressed with the content of the canon of Scripture and especially the mystery doctrine of the Church Age, which is directly pertinent to the believer's spiritual life and the execution of the Protocol Plan of God.

C. Our Lord's Answer Regarding Fasting

Our Lord's answer in verses 34-35 draws on the imagery of a wedding celebration. One cannot make the wedding guests fast while the bridegroom is with them. Everything connected with a wedding is supposed to be related to happiness, dining, and celebration rather than fasting. The days would come when the bridegroom would be taken from them — a reference to the crucifixion and ascension — and then they would fast. But even after the Lord's departure, there is no indication that fasting became a normative spiritual practice.

II. The First Parable — The Garment (Verse 36)

A parable is a short allegorical story designed to convey a truth to believers through an overt sense that even unbelievers can understand, combined with a spiritual meaning represented by the commonplace subject. The parable is an allegory in which the figurative and spiritual meaning of doctrine is described under the guise of a familiar activity of the day.

A. The Analogy Explained

In verse 36, our Lord states: 'No one tears a patch from a new garment and sews it on an old garment. If he does, he will have torn a new garment, and the patch from the new will not match the old.' The Bible must be interpreted in the time in which it was written. At the time of writing, the old garment represented self-righteous arrogance from legalism — the distortion of the Mosaic Law into a system of salvation by works and a spiritual life by works.

True spiritual life is torn or ripped to pieces whenever part of the garment is removed. Furthermore, the spiritual life of the Church does not match up with the Mosaic Law. The dispensation of Israel, which still has seven years to run, was for all practical purposes non-operational during the dispensation of the hypostatic union. The Church Age, and all precedents for the Church Age, is taken from the dispensation of the hypostatic union — Christ's first advent, His salvation work on the cross, His ascension and session at the right hand of the Father — not from the dispensation of Israel.

B. The Problem of Covenant Theology

One of the great problems with many denominational theologies is the failure to distinguish between the unconditional covenants to Israel and the Church. The Abrahamic covenant, Davidic covenant, the real estate covenant (often called the Palestinian covenant), and the new covenant to Israel are unconditional covenants that will be fulfilled — but they are not fulfilled by the Church. This error is called covenant theology. These covenants will be fulfilled after the Church Age, through the tribulation, the second advent of Christ, and the millennial reign. Israel has a future, and the eschatology of Israel is distinct from the eschatology of the Church.

The Mosaic Law, particularly the Ten Commandments, remains the basis for understanding freedom. However, it is not a part of the Christian way of life; it is part of establishment modus operandi. The sacred articles of furniture, the Levitical priesthood, the various sacrifices, and the priestly garments all have significance during the dispensation of Israel, will again have significance in the tribulation, and will have even greater significance in the millennium when Israel is restored as a client nation to God.

C. The Principle Applied

The separation of Israel and the Church is one of the great principles of New Testament interpretation. They are two different elections of God. The true spiritual life is the patch torn from a new garment. That has ruined the new garment, and at the same time it does not match the old garment.

Covenant theology has torn a patch from the Christian way of life and sewn it on the old covenant where it does not match. Precedents for the Christian way of life are taken from our Lord's modus operandi in the dispensation of the hypostatic union rather than the dispensation of Israel. By tearing a patch from the spiritual life of the Church and sewing it onto the Mosaic Law, the believer has torn up the spiritual life of the Church and has sewn it on the Mosaic Law where it has no match.

The two are always kept separate, except that during the Church Age when either Jew or Gentile believes in Jesus Christ, at that moment they enter into union with Christ. As Galatians chapter 3 states, there is no longer a racial issue — 'neither Jew nor Gentile.' Paul said, 'I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ. It is the power of God unto salvation to the Jew first and also to the Gentile.' But once a Jew believes in Jesus Christ in the Church Age, from that time and forever he is separated from Israel as such. He now belongs to the royal family of God.

III. The Second Parable — The Wineskins (Verses 37-38)

The message of this passage is found in the last three verses of Luke chapter 5, where we have the second parable beginning in verse 37.

A. The Grammar of Verse 37

The verse begins literally, 'No one keeps pouring.' The verb is the present active indicative of ballō (βάλλω). While ballō actually means 'to throw,' whenever it is related to wine, the meaning is 'to pour' — wine is not thrown into wineskins but poured. The present tense here is a durative present for an action begun in the past and continuing into the present; past and present are gathered into a single affirmation.

The active voice indicates that the action of the verb is produced by two categories of people. First, the Pharisees — the religious unbelievers who entered into denial and projection. In Matthew 23:24, our Lord called them 'blind guides who strain out a gnat and swallow a camel.' In Matthew 23:27, He said, 'Woe to you, teachers of the law and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you are like whitewashed tombs — beautiful on the outside, but inside full of dead men's bones.'

The second category to whom this applies in the active voice are born-again believers of the Church Age who fail to live their very own spiritual life because of two things: first, wrong priorities that lead to ignorance of God's plan for the Church; and second, denial and projection that hinder application of Bible doctrine to self.

B. The Meaning of the New Wine and Old Wineskins

The corrected translation of verse 37: 'No one makes a habit of pouring new wine into old wineskins.' The new wine in this parable represents the spiritual life of the Church Age believer under the Protocol Plan of God. The new wine is tantamount to hearing the teaching of Bible doctrine but never applying it to oneself. Generally, too much garbage in the heart or the right lobe of the soul is the problem.

When the sin nature controls the soul and a person is listening to the teaching of Bible doctrine, this is like pouring new wine into old wineskins.

C. The Bursting of the Wineskin

Verse 37 continues: 'Otherwise the new wine will burst open the wineskins.' The future active indicative of rhēgnumi (ρήγνυμι) means 'to break open, to burst into pieces, to tear open.' The old wineskin is dried up and has cracks in it. When new wine is poured in, the chemicals in the new wine cause the old wineskin to break open. The wine runs out, and the wineskin is ruined.

The spiritual life of the believer (new wine) is totally incompatible with any system of legalism or self-righteous arrogance. Self-righteous arrogance or legalism is a trend of the sin nature and illustrates the believer entering into the eight stages of reversionism. Any time the believer tries to put the spiritual life God has personally provided as part of the portfolio of invisible assets into the context of legalism or the system of converting outside pressures of adversity into inside pressures of stress in the soul, the wineskin is blown up. It breaks apart, and the spiritual life cannot function in that atmosphere.

D. The Mechanism of Failure

Consider the soul with its ten problem-solving devices on the flat line. Adversity or prosperity presents an outside pressure that breaks through and becomes stress. Stress becomes sin nature control of the soul, resulting in actual sins of the arrogance complex, sins of the emotional complex, and the lust pattern of the sin nature going wild. This leads to one of the trends of the sin nature — either legalism or antinomianism — and from there into the eight stages of reversionism, resulting in Christian moral degeneracy or Christian immoral degeneracy. This is the blowing up of the old wineskin. There is no atmosphere or environment for the spiritual life in relationship to these things.

In the eight stages of reversionism, the wineskin is broken. The 'new' refers to the doctrine of the Church Age related to the spiritual life and the execution of the Protocol Plan. If the believer is in reaction and distraction toward the Word of God, in a frantic search for happiness that neglects doctrine, in Operation Boomerang where everything bounces back into denial and projection, in emotional revolt of the soul, locked in negative volition, blackout of the soul, scar tissue of the soul, or reverse process reversionism — then the spiritual life is destroyed.

Whatever doctrine may have been learned, circulating in the seven compartments of the stream of consciousness, is destroyed once blackout and scar tissue of the soul set in. This is the bursting of the old wineskin. The emphasis in this parable is particularly on Christian moral degeneracy. From this point, the believer often becomes either psychotic or neurotic.

E. Repression, Denial, and Projection

Even if the psychotic status is avoided, the spiritual life is still lost when the wineskin bursts. Three things produce this result:

First, repression — the born-again believer is unable to remember or be cognitively aware of feelings, thoughts, or experiences related to garbage in his own soul. From this comes spiritual denial.

Second, denial — the believer has no perception of reality and fails to acknowledge some aspects of external reality that is obviously apparent to others.

Third, projection — the believer falsely attributes his own unrecognized and unacknowledged sins, failures, flaws, lusts, and antagonistic reactions to others. Projection explains blaming others for one's spiritual condition of stress and garbage in the soul. It explains why so many believers enter into vituperation and vilification, why gossip, maligning, judging, and assigning blame to others for one's own bad decisions becomes the order of the day.

All of these things constitute the old wineskin. The whole concept of repression, denial, and projection is totally incompatible with the spiritual life given to the believer at the moment of salvation. Therefore, it blows up, breaks into pieces, and the spiritual life is gone.

F. New Wine in New Wineskins (Verse 38)

Verse 38 provides the positive principle: 'But new wine must be put into new wineskins.' The new wineskin is the mystery doctrine of the Church Age — the doctrine that is the environment and frame of reference for the spiritual life. Each believer has his very own spiritual life, and each believer alone is the only one who can live that spiritual life. No one else can live it for him. This is why the portfolio of invisible assets gives number one priority to the spiritual life. Each believer must take responsibility for his very own spiritual life. No one else can be blamed for failures; only oneself — which is what the rebound technique acknowledges.

IV. The Distraction Principle (Verse 39)

Verse 39 presents the distraction principle: 'No one drinking the old desires the new.' The word 'wine' is not even used here in the Greek — simply the adjectives 'old' and 'new' are set up in contrast.

A. The Meaning of 'Drinking the Old'

If the believer is involved in repression, denial, and rejection from converting outside pressures of adversity into inside pressures of stress in the soul, he has neither the motivation nor the desire to live his own spiritual life. He may recognize there is a problem and may have heard that he should take in doctrine on a consistent basis, and therefore he comes to listen to Bible doctrine in order to resolve the problem.

But the problem is that he can listen to doctrine all day long and get nothing from it, because he has put new wine in old wineskins, and they break open, and it all pours out. One can listen to Bible doctrine day in and day out, week in and week out, year in and year out, and never grow, because certain things in his life are totally inconsistent with the Protocol Plan of God.

This is why there are always believers who sit under doctrinal teaching, as they did in the days of Moses, and still never advance in the spiritual life. Any time a believer is under stress and has not recovered, the spiritual life is wiped out. The command post is overrun, the spiritual life is put out of action — either there are no problem-solving devices on the flat line of the soul, or they have been destroyed in a previous action.

B. The Mechanism of Distraction

When adversity or prosperity puts the believer out of action because he has converted the pressures into stress, and that stress breaks through the flat line and goes directly to the right lobe of the soul (the heart), it overruns the command post and puts the spiritual life out of action. He can even rebound some sin, but immediately goes right back under the stress principle.

Because of that stress principle, he sits and listens to doctrine having gone into either dissociation or suppression. He has entered into denial and accumulated garbage — not just in the subconscious, but this garbage leaks over into the stream of consciousness. He listens to doctrine but thinks it belongs to someone else, because whenever one is in denial, one goes into projection — taking one's own flaws and projecting them on others.

In the case of a failed relationship, the believer blames the other person. It is not his fault at all; it is their fault. This means he has accumulated garbage, and whenever any doctrine comes up that might apply to that situation, he never applies it to himself. He always applies it to the other person and thinks, 'They ought to hear that. It's too bad they're not here.' He is listening to doctrine but not benefiting from it at all, because he is putting new wine in old wineskins.

C. The Meaning of Chrēstos

Verse 39 concludes: 'For he thinks the old is chrēstos' (χρηστός). This word is in many ways the opposite of agathos (ἀγαθός), meaning 'good of intrinsic value,' which is related to the operational spiritual life.

The best translation for chrēstos in one word would be 'suitable' — but suitable in a specific sense: suitable in the sense of adapting to the purpose of avoiding reality. It is something one can adapt so as to avoid the reality of one's own failure. It could also be translated 'useful' — useful for finding some way of ignoring the responsibility for one's own bad decisions and dealing with them some other way.

This always ends up with garbage in the subconscious, denial, and finding someone to blame. Chrēstos lends itself not only to denial — adapting to the purpose of avoiding reality — but also to the distortion of morality into self-righteousness, exactly as the Pharisees did. This is how our Lord uses this parable in connection with the self-righteousness of the Pharisees who criticized Him for attending Matthew's farewell party.

V. The Parallel in Matthew 9:17

Matthew 9:17 provides a parallel passage with an important addendum: 'Nor do men put new wine in old wineskins. Otherwise, the wineskin bursts apart and the wine rushes out, and the wineskin is ruined. But they put new wine in new wineskins, and both are preserved.'

When the believer puts new wine in new wineskins — when he backs his spiritual life with the metabolization of Bible doctrine, extrapolating from that metabolized doctrine the ten problem-solving devices and putting them on the flat line of the soul — then both the new wine (the spiritual life) and the wineskin (the Protocol Plan of God for the believer's life) are preserved.

The Matthew account emphasizes the preservation of the spiritual life by the new wineskin of metabolized doctrine, by the portfolio of invisible assets, and the problem-solving devices on the flat line. New wine in a new wineskin emphasizes the fact that every born-again believer is a new spiritual species.

Lesson Seven

The Spiritual Life (εὐσέβεια) — Discipline, Nourishment, and Profit

1 Tim. 4:6-8 “If you put these things before the brothers, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, being trained in the words of the faith and of the good doctrine that you have followed. Have nothing to do with irreverent, silly myths. Rather train yourself for godliness; for while bodily training is of some value, godliness is of value in every way, as it holds promise for the present life and also for the life to come.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: In pointing out these things to the brethren, you will be a good servant of Jesus Christ, constantly nourished on the words of the faith and sound doctrine which you have been following. But have nothing to do with worldly fables fit only for old women. On the other hand, discipline yourself for the purpose of your spiritual life. For physical exercise is of some value, but the spiritual life is profitable for all things, having a promise for the present life and for the life to come.

This lesson continues the Spiritual Life Basics series by examining the Greek noun εὐσέβεια (eusebeia), a term systematically mistranslated as 'godliness' in most English versions. Proper exegesis reveals that eusebeia denotes the spiritual life of the Church Age believer—the entire manner of life that flows from adjustment to the justice of God. Three passages in the Pastoral Epistles employ this word to describe both the true spiritual life and its counterfeit. The focus passage, 1 Timothy 4:6-8, establishes the relationship between doctrinal nourishment, self-discipline, and the profit that accrues to the believer who lives within the operational parameters of the spiritual life.

I. The Biblical Term for the Spiritual Life

The Greek noun eusebeia (εὐσέβεια) has been mistranslated as 'godliness' in most English versions. Careful lexical analysis demonstrates that the term actually denotes the Christian faith and life—the spiritual life of the believer. In the Pastoral Epistles, eusebeia designates a particular manner of life, specifically the operational spiritual life that God has assigned to every Church Age believer.

The doctrine of inspiration covers only the original languages of Scripture—Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek. Translations, however numerous, do not carry the same authority. This underscores the importance of the pastor-teacher's gift and his responsibility to be familiar with the original languages. The correct understanding of eusebeia as 'the spiritual life' rather than 'godliness' has far-reaching implications for interpretation of the Pastoral Epistles.

II. The True Versus the False Spiritual Life — 1 Timothy 6:3-6

1 Tim. 6:3-6 “If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not agree with the sound words of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching that accords with godliness, he is puffed up with conceit and understands nothing. He has an unhealthy craving for controversy and for quarrels about words, which produce envy, dissension, slander, evil suspicions, and constant friction among people who are depraved in mind and deprived of the truth, imagining that godliness is a means of gain. But godliness with contentment is great gain.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: If anyone teaches a false doctrine and does not concur with sound doctrine, even the doctrine pertaining to the spiritual life, he has conceded and understands nothing. But he has a morbid, unhealthy mental state interested in controversial questions and disputes about words, out of which arise envy, strife, abusive language, evil suspicions, and constant friction of depraved mind, and deprived of truth, who assume that the spiritual life is a means of profit. But the true spiritual life is a means of great profit when accompanied by contentment.

A. The Rejection of Sound Doctrine

The passage opens with a conditional clause addressing those who teach false doctrine and refuse to concur with sound doctrine—specifically, doctrine pertaining to the spiritual life. When a pastor or congregation accepts false doctrine, they depart from the spiritual life. The reason is given in verse 4: arrogance. The one who rejects true doctrinal teaching 'has conceded and understands nothing.' Arrogance blinds the believer to truth as it is taught from the word of God. Arrogance cannot comprehend Bible doctrine because it is too preoccupied with self.

This is why James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5 both declare: 'God makes war against the arrogant believer, but He gives grace to the humble believer.' Humility is recognition of the authority of the spiritual gift of pastor-teacher. Humility is teachability. Arrogance is not.

B. The Consequences of Rejecting Doctrine

Once a believer rejects Bible doctrine, the outside pressures of adversity and prosperity are converted into inside pressures of stress in the soul. Without the problem-solving devices to protect the soul, the defenses on the forward line of troops are overrun and the spiritual life is put out of action. A vacuum is created in the soul; the stages of reversionism go into operation. The result is described in the second half of verse 4: morbidity, controversial questions, disputes about words, envy, strife, abusive language, evil suspicions.

Verse 5 continues with 'constant friction of depraved mind' and 'deprived of truth.' The believer deprives himself of truth through his own negative volition and wrong priorities. Truth is available; the decision to reject it is a matter of volition. Such believers 'assume that the spiritual life is a means of profit'—but their concept of profit is superficial, usually related to wealth, popularity, or romance. It never occurs to them that God has provided a fantastic system for every circumstance of life that produces genuine happiness.

C. The True Profit of the Spiritual Life

Verse 6 corrects the false assumption: 'But the true spiritual life is a means of great profit when accompanied by contentment.' The word 'contentment' refers to sharing the happiness of God—one of the most powerful problem-solving devices on the forward line of troops. During the dispensation of the hypostatic union, our Lord Jesus Christ in His humanity provided the precedent for our spiritual life. He faced far greater temptations and pressures than we will ever know, yet He maintained perfect happiness in His humanity because He was filled with the Spirit from birth and had the problem-solving devices fully operational.

For the Church Age believer, sharing the happiness of God (the ninth battalion on the forward line of troops) prevents the outside pressures of adversity or prosperity from becoming inside pressure of stress in the soul. When stress penetrates the soul and the sin nature gains control, the command post of the heart is overrun and the spiritual life is put out of action. The true spiritual life is therefore built and constructed on Bible doctrine metabolized and circulating in the stream of consciousness.

III. Discipline for the Spiritual Life — 1 Timothy 4:6-8

A. The Pastor's Responsibility (v. 6)

The passage opens with instruction to Timothy as pastor of the church at Ephesus: 'In pointing out these things to the brethren, you will be a good servant of Jesus Christ, constantly nourished on the words of the faith and sound doctrine which you have been following.' The function of the pastor-teacher is to communicate Bible doctrine. The pastor cannot force anyone to do anything and should not attempt to do so. The believer's motivation must come from Bible doctrine itself. The pastor's responsibility is to nourish the congregation on the words of the faith and sound doctrine.

B. Worldly Fables and False Concepts (v. 7a)

Verse 7 commands: 'But have nothing to do with worldly fables fit only for old women.' Worldly fables are anti-spiritual life concepts—false spiritual life concepts erroneously construed as part of the Christian way of life. These include Christian activism, legalism, cults, and false doctrines of various kinds. The phrase 'fit only for old women' is not antagonistic toward elderly ladies but refers to the historical context of Ephesus—a prosperous city where wealthy widows, having nothing constructive to occupy their time, became sources of wild ideas and problematic influences in the local church.

C. Self-Discipline for the Spiritual Life (v. 7b)

The command continues: 'On the other hand, discipline yourself for the purpose of your spiritual life.' Self-discipline is related to motivation, priorities, and decisions. The Greek verb gumnazō (γυμνάζω), from which we derive 'gymnasium,' means to exercise or train. The believer must train himself for the spiritual life. This involves giving Bible doctrine number one priority: cognition, inculcation, and metabolization of doctrine. The objective is to crowd the garbage out of both the subconscious and the stream of consciousness.

Garbage in the subconscious leaks into the conscious mind and crowds out or distorts Bible doctrine when it is taught. The believer who disciplines himself for the spiritual life addresses this problem systematically through consistent intake of doctrine under the filling of the Spirit.

D. The Analogy of Physical Exercise (v. 8)

Verse 8 provides an analogy: 'For physical exercise is of some value.' The Greek text is clear—physical exercise has value, but limited value. There is a direct relationship between decisions to expose oneself to Bible doctrine and the experience of physical exercise. Good physical exercise involves some discomfort during the process, but afterward there is a sense of well-being. The same principle applies to the spiritual life: consistent exposure to doctrine, though sometimes requiring effort, produces results that are beneficial to the soul.

The analogy is clear: physical exercise benefits the body; the spiritual life benefits the soul. But working out regularly does not make a person spiritual—this is an illustration, not an equation. Physical fitness does not bring one closer to God. What benefits the soul is Bible doctrine metabolized and circulating in the stream of consciousness.

E. The Superior Profit of the Spiritual Life (v. 8b)

The comparison concludes: 'But the spiritual life is profitable for all things, having a promise for the present life and for the life to come.' Physical exercise has limited value—it benefits only the body and only for the present life. The spiritual life, however, is profitable for all things and has promise for both the present life and the life to come.

The word 'promise' is a challenge—the means by which the faith-rest drill is activated. Claiming the promises of God is the starting point for application of doctrine to experience. As Bible doctrine circulates in the stream of consciousness, it develops categorically and produces wisdom. The believer who operates within his spiritual life has promises for both temporal experience and eternal destiny. Utilizing those promises under the outside pressures of adversity or prosperity is essential to the operational function of the spiritual life.

As a born-again believer, one cannot handle the problems of life without the spiritual life being operational. If the spiritual life has been put in mothballs, double discipline results: first, discipline from bad decisions (the law of volitional responsibility), then divine discipline—warning discipline followed by intensive discipline, and eventually, for those who do not recover, dying discipline. But for those who maintain their spiritual life, there is confidence regarding death rather than fear; there is anticipation of being absent from the body and face to face with the Lord.

IV. The Superficial Form of the Spiritual Life — 2 Timothy 3:2-7

2 Tim. 3:2-7 “For people will be lovers of self, lovers of money, proud, arrogant, abusive, disobedient to their parents, ungrateful, unholy, heartless, unappeasable, slanderous, without self-control, brutal, not loving good, treacherous, reckless, swollen with conceit, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, having the appearance of godliness, but denying its power. Avoid such people. For among them are those who creep into households and capture weak women, burdened with sins and led astray by various passions, always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: For believers will be lovers of self, greedy, boastful, arrogant, slanderers, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, unloving, implacable, malicious gossips, without self-control, brutal, not lovers of divine good, treacherous, thoughtless, conceited, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, who hold to a superficial form of the spiritual life but have denied its power. Avoid such persons as these. For among them are those who creep into homes and captivate silly women, weighed down with sins and led on by multifarious lusts, always learning but never able to come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth.

A. A Catalogue of Soul Sins

The context of this final passage provides a checklist of sins into which believers are prone—most of them sins of the soul rather than overt physical sins. The list includes: lovers of self, greedy, boastful, arrogant, slanderers, disobedient to parents (indicating lack of authority orientation from childhood), ungrateful (no capacity for appreciation, often related to repression, denial, and projection), unloving (no capacity for impersonal love), implacable (false forgiveness accompanied by bitterness or jealousy), malicious gossips (vituperation, slander, judging, creating the public lie to destroy reputation), without self-control, brutal, not lovers of divine good, treacherous, thoughtless, conceited.

All of these are illustrations of living outside of one's spiritual life. The spiritual life is part of the believer's portfolio of invisible assets, given at the moment of salvation as one of the forty things God provides for every believer. None of these sins have anything to do with the spiritual life; they are signs that one is not even remotely involved in the spiritual life.

B. Lovers of Pleasure Rather Than Lovers of God

When believers give up on the spiritual life, verse 4 describes their condition: 'lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God.' With no problem-solving devices on the forward line of troops, reversionism replaces the spiritual life. Yet such believers do not want to recognize their condition in these terms. They would claim to have a spiritual life of some sort.

C. The Superficial Form (v. 5)

Verse 5 describes their claim: 'Who hold to a superficial form of the spiritual life but have denied its power.' What is the superficial form? Ritual without reality. Attending on Sunday and calling that the spiritual life with no further exposure—not enough to grow from the teaching of Bible doctrine. There are two powers in the spiritual life: the power of the Holy Spirit (the absolute phase) and the power of the word of God (the relative phase). To deny the power is to have no metabolized doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness through the filling of the Spirit.

Even if some metabolized doctrine remains in the stream of consciousness from previous intake, when fellowship is abandoned and the spiritual life is put out of action, garbage begins leaking in from the subconscious and pushing against whatever doctrine has been learned. Some of it is buried and destroyed; what remains will inevitably be distorted. Bible doctrine requires context, sequence, and coherence. When garbage infiltrates—whether from the subconscious or from acceptance of false doctrine—metabolized doctrine is crowded out of the stream of consciousness.

D. The Command to Avoid Such Persons

The passage commands: 'Avoid such persons as these.' Why? Because these people can have great influence on the believer's life—sometimes subtle, sometimes obvious. They may encourage departure from sound doctrinal teaching with invitations to social activity among believers that substitutes for genuine Christian fellowship. The Bible does not regard social intercourse among believers as Christian fellowship. Christian fellowship in the true sense is what Bible doctrine does to the soul in fellowship with God.

E. Always Learning, Never Arriving (v. 7)

Verse 7 describes the final tragedy: 'Always learning but never able to come to an epignosis (ἐπίγνωσις) knowledge of the truth.' In desperation, some believers recognize that something is wrong and return to Bible class. They expose themselves to doctrine—but they expose themselves as persons with maximum garbage in the subconscious and extensive garbage in the stream of consciousness. Their learning no longer produces the profit it did before because they are trying to learn outside of their spiritual life. This is gnosis without epignosis—academic knowledge in the left lobe that fails to transfer and metabolize in the right lobe.

With no metabolized doctrine in the stream of consciousness and nothing on which to build, such believers must start over with basics almost to recover. The one battalion that has left the forward line but remains nearby to stem the retreat is the rebound technique—the principle of naming known sins to God and recovering fellowship, then continuing forward.

V. Principles of the Spiritual Life

1.. At the very moment of salvation through faith in Jesus Christ, every new believer receives his very own spiritual life tailored to his very own soul and personality.

2.. While every believer receives his own spiritual life at the moment of salvation, the function of that spiritual life is common to all believers. The instructions are uniform—the mandates and principles of Bible doctrine as revealed in the New Testament epistles for the Church Age believer.

3.. The spiritual life possesses both individuality on the one hand and uniformity of modus operandi on the other. The purpose of the spiritual life is never to change one's personality but to give that personality the expression God designed for it.

4.. The individuality of the spiritual life is based on the fact that the believer has an individual soul and personality. At salvation, God the Holy Spirit creates a human spirit as the compatibility basis for communication and teaching of Bible doctrine; God the Father imputes eternal life to that human spirit.

5.. The Holy Spirit teaches the human spirit (Rom. 8:16), which is why the teaching ministry of the Holy Spirit is essential to the spiritual life (John 14:26; 16:12-14; 1 Cor. 2:9-16).

6.. The human soul of the believer is both the location of the spiritual life and the battleground for the spiritual life. The battle is not overt or physical; it is fought in the soul, and it is lost there. A believer can be very moral with regard to the body and still be out of fellowship through self-righteous arrogance.

7.. There are two categories of the spiritual life: the absolute (the filling of the Spirit—either present or absent) and the relative (metabolized doctrine in the soul—a matter of degree based on intake and application).

VI. Applications

1.. You and you alone are the only one who can live your spiritual life. No one else can live it for you.

2.. You and you alone are responsible for your own decisions, good or bad, positive or negative toward doctrine. You cannot blame environment, childhood, people, or anything else for failure to live your own spiritual life.

3.. Your spiritual life functions under divine power only—the power of the Spirit and the power of the word.

4.. Bible doctrine and the filling of the Holy Spirit must have number one priority in life after salvation.

5.. Only through living your very own spiritual life can you glorify God and fulfill His will, purpose, and plan for your life.

6.. You cannot live your spiritual life with garbage in your soul—garbage in the subconscious and garbage in the stream of consciousness. The garbage in the soul dilemma is the subject of the next increment of study in the spiritual life.

Conclusions from Lesson Seven

1.. Eusebeia means the spiritual life. The Greek noun εὐσέβεια, consistently mistranslated as 'godliness,' actually denotes the Christian faith and life—the spiritual life of the Church Age believer as a particular manner of life.

2.. Arrogance is incompatible with the spiritual life. Arrogance blinds the believer to truth, rejects doctrinal teaching, and cannot apply doctrine to self. God makes war against the arrogant but gives grace to the humble.

3.. The true spiritual life produces contentment. When accompanied by sharing the happiness of God, the spiritual life is a means of great profit—not the superficial profit of wealth or popularity, but the genuine profit of capacity for life, love, and happiness.

4.. Self-discipline for the spiritual life parallels physical exercise. Physical exercise has limited value (it benefits only the body for the present life), but the spiritual life is profitable for all things, holding promise for both the present life and the life to come.

5.. The superficial form of the spiritual life denies its power. Ritual without reality, minimal exposure to doctrine, and social activity substituted for fellowship with God through His word constitute the superficial form that lacks both the power of the Spirit and the power of the word.

6.. Gnosis without epignosis is the condition of learning outside the spiritual life. Always learning but never arriving at full knowledge occurs when garbage in the soul prevents the transfer and metabolization of doctrine from the left lobe to the right lobe.

7.. Rebound is the starting point for recovery. The believer who has abandoned the spiritual life must start with naming known sins to God (1 John 1:9) to restore fellowship, then continue forward with doctrinal intake under the filling of the Spirit.

Lesson Eight

The subconscious and stream of consciousness — defense mechanisms vs. problem-solving devices — old garbage and new garbage — the cross as the dividing line

2 Tim. 3:5, 7 “having the appearance of godliness, but denying its power. Avoid such people. ... always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: holding to an outward form of the spiritual life, but having denied its power. ... always learning, but never able to come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth.

This lesson continues the Spiritual Life Basics series by examining why some believers, despite consistent exposure to Bible teaching, experience no spiritual growth. The passage in 2 Timothy 3:5, 7 identifies believers who maintain a superficial form of the spiritual life while denying its power — always learning but never arriving at the metabolized knowledge (epignosis) required for advance. The answer to this dilemma lies in understanding the conflict between defense mechanisms and the problem-solving devices of the protocol plan of God.

I. The Garbage-in-the-Soul Dilemma

The term 'garbage' designates dissociated or repressed thoughts and emotions stored in the subconscious (or, following Pierre Janet's nomenclature, the unconscious). This accumulated material creates what may be called the garbage-in-the-soul dilemma — a difficult and perplexing situation in the believer's spiritual life where unresolved conflicts compete with metabolized doctrine for control of the stream of consciousness.

The word eusebeia (εὐσέβεια) in 2 Timothy 3:5 does not mean merely 'godliness' in the sense of piety. It denotes the Christian faith and life — conduct in relationship to God, manner of life. The believers described in this passage hold to an outward form of this spiritual life but have denied its power (dunamis, δύναμις). The result is a superficial spirituality with no operational capacity.

2 Timothy 3:7 describes the consequence: 'always learning, but never able to come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth.' This is gnosis without epignosis — academic knowledge in the left lobe that is never metabolized and transferred to the right lobe. The failure is not intellectual but spiritual: something in the soul is blocking the normal function of the Grace Apparatus for Perception.

II. The Historical Development: Janet and Freud

Understanding the concept of the subconscious requires a brief survey of its development. Professor Jean-Martin Charcot at the University of Paris trained two famous students: Pierre Janet (1859–1947) and Sigmund Freud. Though both recognized that repressed material exists below the level of conscious awareness, they disagreed on nomenclature and methodology.

A. Pierre Janet and the Subconscious

Janet, a French neurologist and psychologist known for his research in hysteria and neurosis, introduced the term 'subconscious' to categorize traumatic experiences that could not be handled in the stream of consciousness. He theorized that mental forces produce the phenomena manifested in psychoneuroses, and he based his theory on what he called 'psychic tension' — an integrating psychic energy which, when abundant and adequate, holds together the activity of the stream of consciousness through cohesion, producing integration of personality.

When psychic tension becomes deficient — through fatigue, stress, or shock — the integrated elements in the stream of consciousness become defective, resulting in psychoneurosis. Janet proposed that certain mental processes operate in the subconscious independently of or dissociated from the mainstream of consciousness. This concept of dissociation explains how repressed material is buried below conscious awareness.

B. Sigmund Freud and the Unconscious

Freud rejected Janet's term 'subconscious' in favor of 'unconscious,' though both were describing essentially the same phenomenon. Freud began with hypnosis, noting that patients under hypnotic states relived previous emotional experiences, going back to old ideas and impulses they had previously suppressed. After moving to Vienna, Freud abandoned hypnosis in favor of what he called 'talking it out' — a method in which patients spoke freely about whatever came to mind, holding back nothing considered irrelevant or distressing.

Freud observed that past events causing guilt, shame, or emotional disturbance were violently repressed and made unconscious. From this technique of treating psychoneuroses, he developed the term 'psychoanalysis.' For purposes of this study, the term 'subconscious' (Janet's nomenclature) will be used, as Janet remains one of the most significant figures in both psychology and neurology.

III. The Biblical Framework: The Heart

The biblical nomenclature for the right lobe — the seat of thought, decision, and spiritual perception — is the 'heart' (Hebrew: leb/lebab; Greek: kardia). The heart is organized into two functional areas:

(1) The stream of consciousness with its various compartments — the area of conscious thought, decision-making, and the location of metabolized doctrine and the problem-solving devices.

(2) The subconscious — the storage area for repressed thoughts, emotions, and traumatic experiences that have been dissociated from conscious awareness. This is the location of what we are calling 'garbage.'

A critical dynamic in spiritual failure is the 'leak' from the subconscious into the stream of consciousness. When garbage bleeds into the conscious mind, it competes with and often destroys metabolized doctrine, creating a soul conflict that prevents spiritual advance.

IV. Defense Mechanisms Defined

Defense mechanisms are patterns of feeling, thought, and behavior that are relatively involuntary — though Scripture would classify many of them as impulsively voluntary decisions of the sin nature. They arise as reactions to what psychology calls 'psychic danger.' Defense mechanisms are designed to hide or alleviate conflicts, stresses, and the emotional complex of sins: anxiety, hysteria, fear, worry, and guilt.

The following defense mechanisms are pertinent to understanding the garbage-in-the-soul dilemma:

A. Acting Out

A defense mechanism in which a person acts without reflection or apparent regard for negative consequences. Impulsive behavior replaces thoughtful decision-making.

B. Autistic Fantasy

A mechanism in which a person substitutes excessive daydreaming for the pursuit of social relationships. Social interaction through fantasy is easier than direct and effective relationships or the application of problem-solving devices.

C. Denial

A mechanism in which a person fails to acknowledge some aspect of external reality that would be obvious or apparent to others. Denial produces a false perception of reality, often necessary for those who cannot tolerate being wrong and who require constant self-justification. This false perception conflicts with the content of Scripture and with accurate understanding of the sin nature.

D. Dissociation

A mechanism in which a person sustains a temporary alteration in the integrative function of the stream of consciousness or personal identity. Dissociation is the major pipeline into the subconscious — the means by which experiences that cannot be processed in the stream of consciousness are buried. It includes projection and suppression as related functions.

E. Idealization

A mechanism in which a person attributes exaggeratedly positive qualities to self or others. When directed toward others, this is role-model arrogance. When directed toward self, this is self-righteous arrogance.

F. Intellectualization

A mechanism in which a person engages in excessive abstract thinking to avoid reality or to escape disturbing feelings about self.

G. Isolation

A mechanism in which a person is unable to experience simultaneously the cognitive and affective components of an experience because the affective element has been dumped into the garbage of the subconscious. In isolation, there is no leakage of garbage into the stream of consciousness apart from traumatic experience.

H. Projection

A mechanism by which a believer takes his or her own flaws, sins, and failures — of which they are subconsciously but not consciously aware — and attributes them to an object of antagonism. Projection bypasses the stream of consciousness with regard to self while entering the stream of consciousness to assign flaws to someone else. This creates a conflict that both excludes and destroys metabolized doctrine. Projection is 'garbage in, garbage out' — you increase garbage in your own soul while attempting to rid yourself of it.

I. Rationalization

A mechanism in which the believer devises reassuring or self-serving but incorrect explanations for his or her behavior.

J. Repression

A mechanism in which the believer is unable to remember or be cognitively aware of disturbing lusts, wishes, feelings, thoughts, or experiences related to sin.

K. Suppression

A mechanism in which the believer intentionally avoids thinking about disturbing thoughts, problems, experiences, or feelings.

V. Defense Mechanisms vs. Problem-Solving Devices

Defense mechanisms are the function of human volition operating outside the spiritual life. All defense mechanisms come from the same source as sins: the old sin nature. A major soul conflict exists in every born-again believer between defense mechanisms and metabolized doctrine.

Defense mechanisms are no substitute for the function of the ten problem-solving devices in the protocol plan of God. The believer who relies on defense mechanisms rather than problem-solving devices is living outside the spiritual life and cannot advance spiritually.

A. The Critical Questions

The following questions expose the inadequacy of defense mechanisms:

(1) How can a believer have an effective relationship with God apart from the filling of the Spirit, personal love for God the Father, and occupation with Christ? These are three of the great problem-solving devices — the heavy battalions on the right flank of the soul's defensive line.

(2) How can a believer have blessing in relationships with other people without doctrinal orientation and grace orientation?

(3) How can a believer have a life of meaning, purpose, and definition apart from a personal sense of destiny?

(4) How can a believer have the metabolized doctrine necessary to operate the problem-solving devices when functioning with maximum garbage in the soul — both in the stream of consciousness and in the subconscious?

B. The Believer's Option

Every believer has an option in post-salvation experience:

Option One: The function of defense mechanisms to avoid reality, resulting in becoming a loser — even while thinking you are a winner, even while thinking you are always right, even while maintaining a pseudo self-esteem.

Option Two: The function of the ten problem-solving devices on the flat line of the soul to become a winner.

One of these two options will determine whether you are a winner or a loser in the post-salvation experience of the Christian life.

VI. Old Garbage and New Garbage: The Cross as Dividing Line

A factor often overlooked in dealing with the garbage-in-the-soul dilemma is what happens at salvation. At the moment any person believes in Jesus Christ, God does forty things for that person. One of the most critical is the complete removal of all pre-salvation sins and failures.

The cross divides garbage into two categories:

(1) Old Garbage — All sins, failures, reactions to injustice, and traumatic experiences that occurred before salvation.

(2) New Garbage — Sins and failures accumulated after salvation through the function of the old sin nature and defense mechanisms.

A. The Old Garbage Passage: Isaiah 43:25

Isa. 43:25 “I, I am he who blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and I will not remember your sins.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: I, even I, am the one who wipes out your transgressions for my own sake, and I will not remember your sins.

This verse describes what occurs at the moment of salvation. All sins, all failures, all reactions to injustices — everything that accumulated as garbage before faith in Christ — were blotted out. God Himself states that He will not remember these sins.

This means that probing back into pre-salvation childhood to find explanations for present behavior patterns is what may be called 'useless probe.' Why seek to recover what God has permanently blotted out? Two categories of sin are wiped out at the moment of faith in Christ:

(1) Sins committed in childhood, plus reactions to the sins of others (as in the case of an abused child).

(2) Sins committed from childhood to the point of faith in Christ, for those saved as adults.

B. The Special Case of Childhood Abuse

For the abused child — particularly victims of incest or other forms of severe abuse — dissociation and defense mechanisms serve as the only available defense. As a child, the victim is innocent; the sin belongs to the abuser. Yet the trauma produces defense mechanisms that become the child's sanity, the only means of survival.

The problem arises when the child becomes an adult and the defense mechanisms no longer function adequately. However, if that person believes in the Lord Jesus Christ, at that moment everything built up in defense mechanism — everything that happened before salvation — is blotted out. The believer is not called to probe the past but to build the future through the perception and metabolization of Bible doctrine.

Isa. 44:22 “I have blotted out your transgressions like a cloud and your sins like mist; return to me, for I have redeemed you.” (ESV)

The believer must ask: Do I believe these verses? Do I believe that at the moment I trusted in Christ, my entire past was blotted out? If so, why am I probing into the past to find answers instead of probing into the future through perception and metabolization of Bible doctrine?

VII. The Soul Conflict and Reversionism

Garbage in the stream of consciousness (not merely the subconscious) originates from the eight stages of reversionism — specifically from the emotional revolt of the soul, the blackout of the soul, and especially scar tissue of the soul. Scar tissue is formed from the use of defense mechanisms on one hand and the function of sin on the other.

When scar tissue accumulates, there is no capacity for the spiritual life, no capacity for appreciation, no capacity for love or happiness. In the battle in the soul, defense mechanisms are winning. This is the pathway into the eight stages of reversionism.

The same applies to the faith rest drill (the third battalion on the flat line of the soul). The believer who has no spiritual strength to mix the promises of God with faith, or to apply the doctrinal rationales, is powerless and overrun. When stress remains in the soul and combines with defense mechanisms, defense mechanisms win — and the spiritual life is neutralized.

VIII. The Role of Arrogance

Often arrogance acts as a catalytic agent to bring garbage out of the subconscious into the stream of consciousness, where it conflicts with metabolized doctrine. The arrogance complex of sins — self-righteous arrogance, role-model arrogance, denial, rationalization — combines with defense mechanisms to produce pseudo tranquility.

The pattern is: arrogant self-righteousness + defense mechanism = pseudo tranquility. This continues indefinitely because there is no rebound. The believer in arrogant self-righteousness has nothing to rebound because reality has been buried through defense mechanisms. The believer cannot recognize any known sin and therefore cannot utilize the first power of the spiritual life — the filling of the Spirit through rebound.

This is how a believer becomes a loser without recognizing the problem. The statement 'Bible doctrine doesn't work' is not true — the doctrine is not the problem. The problem is the conflict in the soul where defense mechanisms have defeated the problem-solving devices.

Lesson Nine

2 Timothy 3:5, 7 — The Fourth Battle: Defense Mechanisms vs. Problem-Solving Devices — Garbage in the Soul — The Believer's Option for Advance or Regression

2 Tim. 3:5, 7 “having the appearance of godliness, but denying its power. Avoid such people. … always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: Believers who hold to a superficial form of the spiritual life, but they have denied its power. … These believers are always learning, but they never come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth.

This lesson continues the examination of why many believers fail to metabolize Bible doctrine despite consistent exposure to teaching. The previous lesson introduced the concept of garbage in the soul and the role of defense mechanisms. Lesson Nine now addresses the fourth battle of the soul—the conflict between defense mechanisms and the ten problem-solving devices of the Protocol Plan of God. This is the decisive engagement: the believer must replace psychological defense mechanisms with divinely designed problem-solving devices, or the spiritual life remains perpetually dysfunctional.

I. Review: The Structure of the Soul and Its Four Battles

The heart (Hebrew: לֵב, lev; Greek: καρδία, kardia) is the right lobe of the soul, containing the stream of consciousness and the subconscious. This is the location of the spiritual life—where Bible doctrine is metabolized, where decisions are made, and where the battles of the Christian life are fought. The flat line of the soul represents the outer perimeter of defense against external pressures—adversity and prosperity. When these pressures penetrate the flat line, the result is stress in the soul, which is tantamount to sin nature control.

The Four Tactical Conflicts

The soul is the battleground for four sequential conflicts. First, the battle of stress in the soul—the conflict between outside pressures and the resistance of those pressures entering the soul. When this battle is lost, the sin nature assumes control. Second, the battle of sin in the soul—the result of losing the first battle. The worst sins are the arrogance complex, the emotional complex, and the lust pattern of the sin nature, all of which are sins of the soul. Third, the battle of false doctrine in the soul—the result of losing the first two battles. A vacuum is created in the soul that draws in every form of false teaching. Fourth, the battle between defense mechanisms and the ten problem-solving devices of the Protocol Plan of God. This fourth battle is the focus of the present lesson.

II. Defense Mechanisms: Definition and Function

Defense mechanisms are patterns of feelings, thoughts, or behaviors that operate relatively involuntarily as a reaction to psychic danger—what in biblical terms is sin nature control of the soul. They are designed to hide or alleviate the conflicts and stresses that produce anxiety, fear, worry, hysteria, and especially guilt. The categories of defense mechanisms include denial, repression, suppression, projection, rationalization, dissociation, intellectualization, isolation, idealization, and others.

The Problem: Garbage In, Garbage Out

The fundamental problem with defense mechanisms is that they operate on a garbage-in, garbage-out principle. When bitterness accumulates in the soul, for example, the believer suppresses his own flaws into the subconscious (denial, repression). This is 'garbage in.' Then, when conflict arises with another person, the believer projects those suppressed flaws onto the other party—taking his own sins and failures and attributing them to someone else. This is 'garbage out.' The result is gossip, maligning, and judging—all of which are sins of the soul.

Projection is particularly destructive for the self-righteous believer. The person who confuses morality with the Christian life, who maintains a house of cards built on self-righteousness, inevitably uses projection to avoid taking responsibility for personal decisions. The sequence is predictable: bitterness accumulates → flaws are suppressed into the subconscious → denial sets in → conflict with others produces projection of one's own failures onto the other party. This cycle produces neurotic and psychotic believers who never know the sharing of the happiness of God, occupation with the person of Christ, or impersonal love for all mankind.

III. The Two Categories of Garbage in the Soul

Garbage in the soul exists in two distinct locations, each with its own origin and effect on the spiritual life.

A. Garbage in the Subconscious

Garbage in the subconscious originates primarily from repression, dissociation, and denial. This category includes material the believer does not consciously recognize. A critical example is childhood trauma—abuse, incest, torture, or maltreatment at the hands of those in authority. Children who suffer such treatment have no recourse; their only survival mechanism is denial. They suppress the trauma into the subconscious in order to continue functioning under the authority of abusive parents or guardians.

The defense mechanism that saves the child destroys the adult. What was a survival technique in childhood becomes a barrier to spiritual growth in adulthood. The material remains in the subconscious, influencing behavior, producing irrational predilections toward certain sins, and creating pockets of blackout and scar tissue in the soul that prevent the metabolization of Bible doctrine.

B. Garbage in the Stream of Consciousness

Garbage in the stream of consciousness originates from the eight stages of reversionism. This is material the believer may be aware of but refuses to address. It includes the sins of the arrogance complex (jealousy, bitterness, vindictiveness, implacability, vilification, self-pity), the emotional complex, and the lust pattern of the sin nature. When this garbage accumulates, it creates pockets of blackout and scar tissue that prevent doctrine from being received and metabolized.

Both categories—pre-salvation garbage and post-salvation garbage—affect the believer's capacity to learn. The pre-salvation garbage leaves a residual 'smell' that influences the post-salvation life. The post-salvation garbage accumulates through the loss of the four battles in sequence. The result is the condition described in 2 Timothy 3:7: always learning (gnosis in the left lobe), but never able to come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth (metabolized doctrine in the right lobe).

IV. The Fourth Battle: Defense Mechanisms vs. Problem-Solving Devices

The fourth battle is described as garbage versus metabolized doctrine in the stream of consciousness. When a believer has not learned doctrine—and all categories of doctrine are essential—there is nothing from which to extrapolate the ten problem-solving devices. The result is that the believer defaults to defense mechanisms to cope with the pressures of life.

The ten problem-solving devices are designed to function on the flat line of the soul, defending against external pressures. But they also have 'detachments' that deal with all four internal battles. There is no problem in the spiritual life for which there is no solution. The Bible provides a solution for everything—but the believer must learn the doctrine, form the problem-solving devices in his soul, and deploy them in the privacy of his own priesthood.

The Sequence of Deployment

The first battalion is the rebound technique (1 John 1:9)—without it, the believer cannot recover fellowship with God or the filling of the Spirit. The second battalion is the filling of the Spirit. The third and fourth are grace orientation and doctrinal orientation. When these four are operational, the believer develops a personal sense of destiny. With a personal sense of destiny established, no circumstance in life—no matter how adverse—can penetrate the soul and destroy the spiritual life.

V. Principle: Defense Mechanisms Are No Substitute for Problem-Solving Devices

The first major principle of this lesson is that defense mechanisms are no substitute for the function of the spiritual life. Specifically, defense mechanisms must never replace the function of the ten problem-solving devices on the flat line of the soul. This principle recognizes the distinction between the child victim of abuse—who must use denial to survive—and the adult believer—who must replace that denial system with divinely designed mechanisms for handling life.

The defense mechanism that saves the child destroys the adult. After salvation, the believer must shed the survival systems of childhood and replace them with the problem-solving devices extrapolated from metabolized doctrine. As long as the believer clings to defense mechanisms, he will remain divorced from reality, unable to see himself in the mirror of the Word of God (James 1), and unable to advance toward spiritual maturity.

VI. The Believer's Post-Salvation Options

The second major principle is that the believer has an option in the post-salvation experience. On the one hand is the option of advance; on the other, the option of regression through defense mechanisms.

Option One: Advance — Philippians 3:13–14

Phil. 3:13–14 “Brothers, I do not consider that I have made it my own. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: Brethren, I do not regard myself as having laid hold of the objective, but one thing I do: forgetting what is behind and advancing toward what is ahead, I press on toward the objective for the prize of the upward call from God in Christ Jesus.

'Forgetting what is behind' is the great challenge. What is behind may include childhood trauma, teenage failures, and the accumulated garbage of the pre-salvation and post-salvation life. The believer who advances does not sit in perpetual self-examination wondering whether he has achieved maturity. Instead, he presses toward the objective, leaving the past behind.

Option Two: Regression — 2 Timothy 3:5, 7

The alternative to advance is the condition described in the focus passage: believers who hold to a superficial form of the spiritual life but have denied its power. The two powers of the spiritual life are the power of the Holy Spirit and the power of the Word of God. The energy of the flesh is no more successful in executing the Christian life than it was at the point of salvation. These believers are always learning (gnosis) but never able to come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth—never able to metabolize doctrine because garbage in the soul blocks its reception.

VII. Policy Principles Regarding Therapy and the Spiritual Life

Before proceeding further into the doctrine of the spiritual life, certain policy principles must be established regarding the relationship between psychotherapy, psychiatry, and the believer's spiritual life.

A. Recognition of Legitimate Medical Help

Psychotherapy and psychiatry are not a part of the spiritual life as such, but this does not exclude their benefits in certain cases. The advances in psychiatric medication represent some of the greatest achievements in the history of medicine. Believers suffering from manic-depressive disorder, schizophrenia, or other conditions cannot function without proper medication. Medication has made it possible for neurotic and psychotic believers to function to the extent of absorbing Bible doctrine.

B. Three Foundational Principles

First, the defense mechanism that saves the child destroys the adult. Second, after salvation through faith in Christ, defense mechanisms are no substitute for the extrapolation of the ten problem-solving devices. Third, you and you alone are the only one who can live your spiritual life. No one else can live it for you. Counseling, in a sense, is walking on crutches—people may take advice for a day or two, but it does not carry them anywhere. The Christian life is designed for the believer to solve his own problems in the privacy of his own priesthood.

C. Principles for Those Seeking Therapy

Some believers may need therapy because of extreme or unusual traumas in their past. Others may need help to cope with specific situations. Others do not need it at all. The following principles govern the decision:

(1) The decision must be left to the individual involved. The fewer people who know about the problem outside of professional help, the better. (2) The goal of the therapy process is change—not to establish blame or prevent the function of the spiritual life. (3) When the process of therapy becomes an end rather than a means, the believer may become bogged down in the past to the detriment of his spiritual life.

D. Past Events and Self-Evaluation

Evaluation of past events may help the believer avoid future mistakes. Unfortunately, many believers refuse to learn from their own past failures—just as many people in the nation have failed to learn from history. Past events should never be evaluated for self-justification or to assign blame to someone else in projection. As long as a believer is blaming anyone for what is happening in his life, bitterness remains in the soul—and bitterness is among the most devastating of all sins. Any injustice or unfairness, real or imagined, if retained as bitterness, will prevent the execution of the spiritual life.

VIII. The Sacred Responsibility: The Spiritual Life Is a Divine Monopoly

The believer's spiritual life is stated dogmatically and unequivocally in the New Testament epistles as a divine monopoly for the execution of God's will, God's plan, and God's purpose for the believer's life. God can only be glorified through maximum metabolized doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness and ten problem-solving devices on the flat line of the soul.

The spiritual gift of pastor-teacher communicates the doctrine. The doctrine goes to the human spirit (pneuma), where the Holy Spirit teaches the human spirit, then sends it to the left lobe as gnosis. But gnosis is like food on a table—it does not sustain life by being observed. It must be metabolized into the heart as epignosis. From epignosis the believer extrapolates the ten problem-solving devices and deploys them on the flat line. This is the divinely designed fuel system for the Christian life. Using anything else—the energy of the flesh, defense mechanisms, human works—is like putting water in a gas tank.

9. The spiritual life is a divine monopoly. Only the believer can live his own spiritual life. Counseling is walking on crutches. Lesson Ten

The Vacuum of the Mind — Four Tactical Battles in the Soul — Garbage Classification

Eph. 4:17 “Now this I say and testify in the Lord, that you must no longer walk as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their minds.” (ESV)

This lesson concludes the introductory framework for the Spiritual Dynamics series by examining the four great tactical conflicts that occur within the soul. The key passage, Ephesians 4:17, introduces the concept of the vacuum of the mind — the condition that results when a believer loses the successive battles of stress, sin, and false doctrine in the soul. Understanding these battles and the resulting garbage accumulation prepares the believer to recognize the stakes involved in daily decisions regarding Bible doctrine and fellowship with God.

I. Review: Foundational Principles of the Spiritual Life

Before examining the tactical conflicts in the soul, seven foundational principles establish the framework for understanding the spiritual life in the Church Age.

A. Principle One: Individual Responsibility

You and you alone are the only one who can live your spiritual life. No one else can live it for you. You will encounter people who attempt to live your spiritual life for you — generally people who are not doing a good job living their own. They are often legalistic, full of self-righteous arrogance, subjective, and operating under the power and approbation lust of the sin nature. There is no substitute for sitting under the privacy of your own priesthood and receiving the teaching of the Word of God.

B. Principle Two: Personal Accountability

Consequently, you and you alone are responsible for your own decisions. As an adult believer, you have been given by God — prepared in eternity past — your very own spiritual life. This spiritual life is not designed to change your basic personality. You are responsible for your decisions, good or bad, positive or negative toward Bible doctrine. You cannot blame environment, background, or anyone else for your failure to execute your Christian life. It is between you and the Lord under the doctrine of the universal priesthood of the believer.

C. Principle Three: Divine Power Only

Your spiritual life was designed by God in eternity past to function under divine power only, not human power. This is the grace principle operating through two categories of divine power: (1) the power of the Holy Spirit — believers are mandated to be filled with the Spirit (Eph. 5:18) and to walk by means of the Spirit (Gal. 5:16); and (2) the power of the Word of God — believers are mandated to give Bible doctrine and Bible teaching a hearing.

D. Principle Four: Priority of Doctrine

Since the filling of the Holy Spirit plus cognition, inculcation, metabolization, and application of Bible doctrine are mandated for all believers, your scale of values must be revised to assign number one priority to the perception of Bible doctrine. Once these mandates become part of your understanding of the Word, it is your responsibility to reassess your priorities accordingly.

E. Principle Five: Glorifying God

Only through living your very own spiritual life, through utilization of divine power, can you ever glorify God. It is irrational to suppose that anything done in the energy of the flesh can glorify God. God has provided a complete portfolio for each believer, making it possible to utilize His provision to execute the Protocol Plan for the Church and glorify Him thereby.

F. Principle Six: Location in the Soul

The heart — the right lobe of the soul — is the location for your spiritual life. While your spiritual life was assigned to you at the moment of salvation through faith in Christ, it was prepared for you by God the Father in eternity past. Therefore your spiritual life is older than creation, older than human history, older than the prehistoric angelic conflict. Your spiritual life pre-existed you by an infinite number of years.

G. Principle Seven: Four Tactical Conflicts

The soul is the location of four great tactical conflicts in the spiritual life: (1) the battle of stress in the soul, (2) the battle of sin in the soul, (3) the battle of false doctrine in the soul, and (4) the conflict with defense mechanisms. These four battles form a domino sequence — losing the first leads to losing the second, and so forth.

II. The First Battle: Stress in the Soul

The battle of stress in the soul is the direct attack of outside pressures — both adversity and prosperity — seeking tactical penetration of the flat line of the soul. The function of the ten problem-solving devices on the flat line is designed to prevent this penetration.

A. The Flat Line Defense

When a believer begins the spiritual life, the flat line is sparsely defended. The first problem-solving device learned is rebound — the recovery mechanism. Then comes the filling of the Spirit, followed by the faith-rest drill. As the believer grows, additional problem-solving devices are developed: occupation with Christ, impersonal love for all mankind, and others. But early in the spiritual life, there are exposed flanks.

B. Divine Protection of the Flank

When a believer has not yet learned enough doctrine to cover the exposed flank, God in His fairness provides protection. First Corinthians 10:13 states that God will not permit testing beyond the believer's capability. God protects the flank while the believer develops additional problem-solving devices through the extrapolation of metabolized doctrine. This is the best protected flank in the world — God Himself stands guard while you are developing.

C. Two Categories of Outside Pressure

There are two categories of outside pressure that attack the soul: adversity and prosperity. There are eleven categories under adversity and five under prosperity. With the ten problem-solving devices functioning on the flat line, there will be no penetration unless the believer is out of fellowship and not directing these defensive positions.

D. The Worst Casualty

The worst casualty in the spiritual life is the conversion of outside pressure into inside pressure — stress in the soul. Stress in the soul means sin nature control, a very tenacious control that makes recovery difficult. When stress penetrates the soul, all spiritual strength is lost. You may know promises and how to claim them, you may know doctrinal rationales and how to use them, but you are too weak to apply them. This is the unhappy hour of your life.

E. The Emotional Complex of Sins

When stress penetrates and the faith-rest drill retreats from the flat line, the emotional complex of sins takes over. There are five categories, ranging from fear, worry, anxiety, and trepidation to guilt and shame. This emotional takeover renders the believer irrational and can keep the spiritual life out of action for an extended period.

F. Recovery Procedure

Recovery requires counterattack with rebound and the filling of the Spirit. The filling of the Spirit demands the resurgence of metabolized doctrine from the seven compartments of the stream of consciousness — this is spiritual muscle. From the command post of the soul, the problem-solving devices must be returned to the flat line. God intends for the believer under maximum outside pressure to have the same tranquil life as when everything is going well. The peace that passes all understanding should characterize both the greatest adversities and the greatest moments of prosperity.

III. The Second Battle: Sin in the Soul

The battle of sin in the soul is the result of losing the first battle. If you have lost the battle of stress in your soul tactically, you will lose the battle of sin in the soul. This battle results from two tactical situations.

A. Sins of Cognizance and Ignorance

The believer is using volition to commit both sins of cognizance and sins of ignorance. Volition is involved in both categories, and God holds the believer equally responsible for sins of ignorance as for sins of cognizance. The principle is: you wanted to do it and you did it.

B. Exploitation by the Sin Nature

As a result of losing the first battle — outside pressure becoming inside stress — the sin nature exploits and annihilates the spiritual life through stress and sin nature domination. The three categories of soul sins — the arrogance complex, the emotional complex, and the lust pattern — are by far the worst sins. The seven sins that God abominates include only one overt sin (homicide); three deal with mental attitude and three with sins of the tongue.

IV. The Third Battle: False Doctrine in the Soul

As a result of losing the first two battles, you will lose the third battle. There is a domino effect. A vacuum has opened up as a result of losing those first two battles.

A. The Vacuum of the Mind

Eph. 4:17 “Now this I say and testify in the Lord, that you must no longer walk as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their minds.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: Therefore I communicate this doctrine, and because of the Lord — that is, the precedent established by the humanity of Christ in the dispensation of the hypostatic union — I insist that you no longer walk even as unbelievers walk, in the vacuum of their mind.

One of the greatest battlefield casualties in the spiritual life is the vacuum created by losing the first two battles. This vacuum functions like a giant vacuum cleaner — anything false, inappropriate, or wrong is sucked into the soul. Every system of false doctrine is attracted into the vacuum.

B. Results of the Vacuum

The vacuum in the mind not only attracts every system of false doctrine but also results in seeking solutions through self-righteous arrogance, legalism, and the irrationality of emotional revolt of the soul. Born-again believers get caught up in false movements and learn anti-biblical phrases and concepts. Once false doctrine enters, it gives muscle to the garbage already in the soul.

V. The Fourth Battle: Defense Mechanisms

The fourth conflict is the result of losing the first two battles plus retreat and confusion of the soul from being set back by the third battle. Now that false doctrine is in the soul along with garbage, the fourth battle becomes a soul conflict of garbage versus metabolized doctrine.

A. Two Categories of Garbage Accumulation

When the believer is losing the fourth battle, two categories of garbage accumulate in the soul:

First, garbage in the subconscious — related to defense mechanisms such as suppression, repression, dissociation, and denial. Out of dissociation come the most severe problems, including multiple personality disorders.

Second, garbage in the stream of consciousness — related to idealization and role model arrogance. This includes creating a role model, discovering their flaws, destroying them, and then creating a role model out of yourself. It also includes autistic fantasy tantamount to unrealistic expectation — expecting attention, socialization, and recognition from a church rather than focusing on learning doctrine and growing spiritually.

B. The Pattern of Garbage In and Garbage Out

A losing battle is perpetuated through the pattern of garbage in and garbage out. Several illustrations demonstrate this pattern:

Pattern One: Garbage in — repression and denial, resulting in no perception of reality regarding self. Garbage out — projection, which assigns blame to others and superimposes one's own flaws onto them while remaining oblivious to the source of those assigned flaws.

Pattern Two: Garbage in — reaction to rejection through arrogance principles like self-pity, bitterness, or the emotional complex of sins. Garbage out — projection as the reversal of the faith-rest drill.

Pattern Three: Garbage in the stream of consciousness — bitterness and jealousy that are consciously present (not suppressed into the subconscious). Garbage out — justifying the sins of vituperation and vilification, justifying malice and revenge.

C. Trashing the Stream of Consciousness

While defense mechanisms bury garbage in the subconscious, they also trash the stream of consciousness. The seven compartments of the stream of consciousness — where metabolized doctrine should reside — become littered with garbage from the eight stages of reversionism. There is conflict in each compartment, meaning that even metabolized doctrine faces resistance. It takes an overwhelming amount of doctrine, patience, and consistent rebound to eventually clear the trash from the soul. The longer the believer has been out of fellowship, the more trash accumulates. But metabolized doctrine will clean it out — including the subconscious.

D. The Critical Compartments

Among the first compartments to be damaged are categorical storage (the third compartment) and doctrinal categories (the fourth compartment). When these are compromised, both hands are tied behind your back in the fight. This is why believers enter the cosmic system — cosmic one and cosmic two — through the vacuum of the mind.

E. Hope for Recovery

You are never out of the battle as long as you are alive. You can always counterattack tactically. God, who reads your mind and knew simultaneously all decisions in eternity past, provides extra time based on His knowledge of your intentions. You will never depart this life without having had equal privilege and equal opportunity to execute God's plan, will, and purpose for your life. God keeps the believer alive because He knows the intention, and once that intention reverses toward doctrine, He knows the end from the beginning.

VI. Garbage Classification

In understanding the spiritual life, it is essential to identify what constitutes garbage in the soul. Seven categories define garbage:

Category 2 Garbage related to the sins of the emotional complex
Category 3 Garbage related to the lust pattern of the sin nature
Category 4 Garbage related to stress in the soul
Category 5 Garbage related to the blackout of the soul

A. The Lust Pattern

The lust pattern of the sin nature (Category 3) includes multiple expressions: power lust, approbation lust, social lust, pleasure lust, sexual lust, chemical lust (drugs and alcohol), monetary lust (greed and avarice), criminal lust, and crusader lust. Crusader lust involves believers in activism and civil disobedience, destroying the distinction between church and state and distracting from the true objective — the execution of the Protocol Plan of God.

B. The Unifying Principle

All garbage in the soul is related to the essence of the sin nature. The sin nature has an area of weakness (the source of sins through volition), an area of strength (producing human good and dead works), and trends toward legalism or antinomianism. Legalism results in self-righteous arrogance; antinomianism results in sexual sins, criminal sins, and chemical sins. Each of these is a gate leading to the eight stages of reversionism. The lust pattern also includes malice — the tremendous lust to destroy, injure, or hurt someone else.

Conclusions from Lesson Ten

1. Individual responsibility governs the spiritual life. You alone can live your spiritual life. No one else can live it for you. Those who attempt to control your spiritual life are generally failing in their own and operating under the arrogance and lust of the sin nature.

Environment, background, and other people cannot be blamed for failure to execute the Christian life. It is between you and the Lord under the universal priesthood of the believer.

4. Doctrine must receive number one priority. Because the filling of the Spirit plus cognition, inculcation, metabolization, and application of Bible doctrine are mandated, your scale of values must be revised to assign first priority to the perception of Bible doctrine.

5. Glorifying God requires divine power. Only through living your spiritual life by utilization of divine power can you glorify God. Nothing done in the energy of the flesh glorifies God.

6. The spiritual life has its location in the heart. The heart — the right lobe of the soul — is the location for your spiritual life. This spiritual life was prepared in eternity past, making it older than creation, human history, and the angelic conflict.

7. Four tactical battles determine spiritual success or failure. The soul is the battleground for four conflicts: stress, sin, false doctrine, and defense mechanisms. These battles form a domino sequence — losing one leads to losing the next.

10. Seven categories define garbage. All garbage in the soul relates to the essence of the sin nature: arrogance complex, emotional complex, lust pattern, stress, blackout of the soul, scar tissue, and defense mechanisms.

Lesson Eleven

James 1:20–25 · The Battle of Sin in the Soul · Self-Righteous Arrogance and Iconoclastic Arrogance · The Mirror of the Word · Doers versus Hearers · gnosis / epignosis · The Recovery Process

Therefore remove all pollution and excess of evil and receive with humility the implanted doctrine, which is able to deliver your souls. Moreover, keep on becoming doers of the word and not hearers only, constantly deluding yourselves. For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, this flawed person is like a believer who looks at his natural face in a mirror. Within the Spiritual Life Basics series, this lesson addresses the second of the four battles in the soul — the battle of sin — and demonstrates how self-righteous arrogance blocks the metabolization process that produces spiritual growth. James 1:20–25 provides the theological framework: the believer must approach Bible doctrine with humility, function as a doer of the word rather than a hearer only, and use the word as a mirror to see accurately what manner of person he or she is. The passage exposes the profile of the flawed believer whose self-righteous arrogance prevents the transfer of doctrine from the left lobe to the right lobe — from academic knowledge to metabolized truth.

I. The Battle of Sin — Not from a State of Perfection

In the previous lessons we identified the four battles in the soul: the battle of stress, the battle of sin, the battle of false doctrine, and the battle of defense mechanisms. The battle of sin in the soul demands a critical qualification: we do not battle sin from a state of perfection. Many believers fail miserably because of self-righteous arrogance — the locked-in arrogance that characterized Israel under great Bible teaching throughout the Old Testament and that now exists in believers who have never realized that their first responsibility to God is consistent intake of Bible doctrine.

The apostle John emphasizes this reality in two parallel statements:

1 John 1:8 “If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: If we say that we have no sin, we are deceiving ourselves, and the truth is not in us.
1 John 1:10 “If we say we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: If we say that we have not sinned, we make Him a liar, and His word is not in us.

Notice the double indictment: if a believer claims sinlessness, the truth is not in that believer, and God's word is not in that believer. The battle of sin in the soul is fought from the status quo of fellowship with God — the operational sphere restored and maintained through rebound. The only Person who ever battled temptation from a state of perfection was the humanity of Christ in hypostatic union. For all other believers, the loss of fellowship through sin is inevitable; therefore, rebound is the first battalion on the flat line of the soul, and its consistent use is non-negotiable for spiritual advance.

II. Iconoclastic Arrogance — The Feet of Clay Syndrome

Iconoclastic arrogance is defined as subjective preoccupation with other people — eyes on people — resulting from disappointment, disenchantment, or disillusion. This pattern may be termed the 'feet of clay syndrome.' It operates as a process with identifiable stages.

A. Creation of the Role Model

The believer begins by creating a role model out of another person — a friend, a romantic interest, an admired figure. This amounts to placing that person on a pedestal, which is tantamount to creating an idol from one's own volition. The only legitimate role model in the spiritual life is the Lord Jesus Christ; occupation with Christ is the heaviest battalion on the flat line. When a believer substitutes a human role model for Christ, tactical loss in the soul battles has already begun.

B. Loss of Reality Through Defense Mechanisms

The role model or idol is inflated through the defense mechanism of idealization: the believer attributes exaggeratedly positive qualities to the person on the pedestal. This inflation is extended through autistic fantasy, in which excessive daydreaming substitutes for effective problem-solving. Reality is sacrificed to maintain the idol.

C. The Feet of Clay Revelation

Eventually the believer observes the 'feet of clay' — the inevitable flaws in any human being. Disillusion, disappointment, and denial follow. The creator of the idol — the believer operating in self-righteous arrogance — now reacts with vicious iconoclasm: maligning, judging, gossiping, vindictiveness, malice, and a lust for revenge. The idol that was created in arrogance is destroyed in arrogance.

D. Tactical Defeat in Three Battles

Iconoclastic arrogance utilizes the sins of the emotional complex, the sins of the arrogance complex, and categories of lust from the sin nature. The result is tactical defeat in three of the four battles of the soul: the battle of stress, the battle of sin, and the battle of defense mechanisms. The believer is now outside the operational sphere of the spiritual life.

III. The Ultimate Pedestal — Role Model of Self

When a believer cycles through iconoclastic arrogance repeatedly — placing successive people on pedestals and destroying them — a final development occurs: the believer constructs a large pedestal and places self upon it. This is the role model of self, a system of pseudo self-esteem in which the believer considers himself very strong when in reality he is very weak.

This pseudo self-esteem operates through two false reactions: (1) denial and projection, and (2) inflated self-assessment. Neither can win the battle between defense mechanisms and problem-solving devices or the battle of stress in the soul. The believer who has made a role model of self is locked in unrealistic expectation: self-righteous arrogance with its inflated view of self expects everyone to share that view. When this does not occur, unrealistic expectation produces frustration and unhappiness, resulting in projection — blaming others for one's own miserable state.

Three principles govern this pattern:

(1) The unrealistic expectation of the believer in repression and denial not only blames others for his or her miserable state but becomes aggressive under legalism to change others to conform to his or her unrealistic expectations.

(2) Unrealistic expectation plus denial and repression never realizes that in the spiritual life there is only one person you can change — yourself.

(3) You and you alone are the only one who can live your spiritual life; no one else can live it for you.

The believer who reaches this stage — role model of self constructed from accumulated garbage — is on the tragic road to maximum divine discipline. Such believers may be kept alive for a time so that advancing believers may practice the problem-solving devices in their presence, but the trajectory is toward the sin unto death.

IV. Impersonal Love — The Realistic Alternative

The antidote to iconoclastic arrogance is impersonal love. Personal love emphasizes the attractiveness of the object: 'I love you' focuses on the object. This emphasis inevitably creates an image and leads to idealization. Impersonal love, by contrast, emphasizes the virtue of the subject: the believer's own integrity, not the qualities of the object. The object can be the most obnoxious person in town, and impersonal love functions regardless.

Impersonal love is a problem-solving device, a heavy battalion on the flat line of the soul. It fulfills the divine mandate to love all mankind — a mandate that cannot be fulfilled through personal love, which is impossible to extend to everyone. The field of impersonal love is the field of realism; the field of personal love without the prior foundation of impersonal love is the field of idealization and eventual iconoclasm.

V. James 1:20–25 — The Mirror of the Word

A. Verse 20: Anger Does Not Achieve Virtue

James begins with a principle: 'For the anger of a person does not achieve virtue from God.' The Greek noun orgē (ὀργή) refers to the settled anger of implacability and vindictiveness — the characteristic anger of self-righteous arrogance. This is not the flash of temper but the sustained anger that one calls 'justified.' Such anger never produces righteousness from God's perspective.

B. Verse 21: The Two Prerequisites

Verse 21 presents the two prerequisites for spiritual recovery:

(1) 'Remove all pollution and excess of evil.' No problem-solving begins until the believer acquires the first two battalions on the flat line — rebound and the filling of the Spirit. The 'pollution' includes the sins of the arrogance complex and the emotional complex that characterize iconoclastic arrogance.

(2) 'Receive with humility the implanted doctrine, which is able to deliver your souls.' The Greek noun praÿtēs (πραὐτης), translated 'meekness' in the ESV and 'humility' in the corrected translation, denotes the teachable attitude that is the prerequisite for all metabolization. No one learns Bible doctrine in arrogance. The filling of the Spirit is humility — 'God makes war against the arrogant believer, but He gives grace to the humble believer.'

Note the phrase 'implanted doctrine' — this is doctrine that has taken root in the soul, doctrine that has moved from the left lobe to the right lobe. Such doctrine is able to 'deliver your souls' — not in the sense of salvation adjustment but in the sense of deliverance from the enslaving patterns of the sin nature and defense mechanisms.

C. Verses 22–24: Hearers Versus Doers

Verse 22 establishes the contrast between hearers and doers:

'Keep on becoming doers of the word and not hearers only, constantly deluding yourselves.' The 'doer of the word' is the believer with maximum metabolized doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness. The 'hearer of the word' is the believer who gives doctrine a hearing and goes only as far as gnosis (γνῶσις) — academic knowledge in the left lobe. Hearing alone is not enough; doctrine must be metabolized.

The self-righteous arrogance believer can be a hearer of the word but is never a doer of the word. Such a believer may engage in Christian activity — witnessing, service — but produces dead works, not the gold, silver, and precious stones of genuine production.

Principle: No epignosis (επίγνωσις) in the right lobe means a flawed believer who cannot apply doctrine to self and distorts doctrine into application to others.

Verses 23–24 describe the hearer-only believer with a memorable illustration: this flawed person is like one who looks at his natural face in a mirror, then departs and immediately forgets what kind of person he was. The believer who is a hearer but not a doer looks into the mirror of the word of God, listens to what he wants to hear, shuts out the rest through defense mechanisms, and never sees himself as he truly is. The subconscious accumulation of garbage — from iconoclastic arrogance and other patterns — becomes a pedestal of denial and unreality.

D. Verse 25: The Perfect Law of Freedom

Verse 25 presents the contrast — the believer who advances:

'But he who looks intently into the perfect law of freedom and has persisted — not having become a forgetful hearer but a doer of accomplishment — this believer will be happy in what he does.'

The 'perfect law of freedom' is the word of God as experienced by the advancing believer. The believer is free within the divine dynasphere to metabolize doctrine, to move to spiritual self-esteem, to produce divine good, and eventually to take the high ground of spiritual maturity.

The Greek verb paramenō (παραμένω), rendered 'has persisted,' is an intensive perfect tense — the believer has been persisting over a period of time with the result that the persistence is now paying dividends. This is not instant growth but the cumulative effect of consistent intake.

The outcome: 'this believer will be happy in what he does.' This is the eighth battalion on the flat line — sharing the happiness of God. The believer who looks intently into the mirror, receives doctrine with humility, metabolizes it consistently, and applies it accurately will experience the +H of divine happiness.

VI. Humility — The Prerequisite for Perception

The humility required for metabolization is directly related to recognition of legitimate authority. One of the great tragedies — whether from child abuse or other family dysfunction — is the destruction of the first authority a person encounters: parental authority. When that authority is destroyed before a child's eyes, the capacity for humility is severely damaged.

Legitimate authority exists in every sphere of life: the husband in marriage, the parents in the family, the coach on the athletic team, the teacher in the classroom, the police officer in civil society, the officers in business, senior rank in the military. Authority in life is essential because no one can learn without it. The believer who approaches the teaching of the word of God without humility will make an issue out of the pastor's personality rather than the content of the message, or will become a busybody critiquing everything except his own soul.

When humility is present, the believer can receive the implanted doctrine. When self-righteous arrogance is present, the believer has selective hearing — hearing only what stimulates self-righteousness, picking up only what can be used to criticize or manipulate others. The self-righteous arrogance person cannot see himself in the mirror of the word; the reflection is distorted by the pedestal of denial.

VII. The Road to Recovery

The passage provides the recovery process for the flawed believer:

(1) Remove the pollution — rebound, restore fellowship, acquire the filling of the Spirit.

(2) Receive doctrine with humility — not with arrogance, not with selective hearing, not with the defense mechanism of denial.

(3) Look intently into the mirror of the word — see yourself as you actually are, not as you imagine yourself to be on the pedestal of self.

(4) Persist — continue in the metabolization process over time; there are no instant solutions.

(5) Become a doer of accomplishment — apply metabolized doctrine from the right lobe, not academic information from the left lobe.

The garbage in the subconscious can be overcome — not instantly, but through consistent metabolization. Garbage in the stream of consciousness is dealt with first; as metabolized doctrine accumulates, it displaces and overcomes the garbage in the subconscious. The process requires the hard road back, taken in the privacy of the priesthood, between the individual believer and the Lord.

I. The Context: Divine Authority in Times of Adversity

The phrase "Thus says the Lord" establishes a foundational principle for the entire passage: what God has to say about any situation is infinitely more important than what any human being has to say. In times of national crisis, the human tendency is to look for a person — a leader, a political figure, a demagogue — to provide solutions. This tendency represents a fundamental failure to recognize that only God has solutions for times of adversity. The divine origin of this passage establishes its authority as the definitive word on spiritual stability in crisis conditions.

Jeremiah's ministry occurred during the decline and fall of the southern kingdom of Judah, the fourth client nation to God. Judah was experiencing the same patterns of national degeneration that characterize any client nation moving toward the fifth cycle of discipline: widespread moral and immoral degeneracy, anti-military sentiment, economic destruction, false doctrine from religious leaders who proclaimed "peace, peace" when there was no peace, and a general abandonment of the principles of divine establishment that had made the nation great. In the midst of this national collapse, Jeremiah taught Bible classes to a generation of young people who would survive the fifth cycle and become the nucleus for a future restoration of Israel. Jeremiah 17:5-8 was one of the shorter doctrinal lessons that equipped these young believers for the most famous death march in history — the Babylonian captivity.

II. The Tumbleweed Believer: Negative Volition and the Sin Nature

A. The Principle of Volitional Responsibility

The passage begins with a statement of divine cursing, but this cursing operates according to the law of volitional responsibility. No nation is ever destroyed by an outside power as the primary cause — though an outside power is always the final instrument of destruction. The nation is always destroyed from within. It is what happens inside the nation that determines its fate. The accumulated negative volition and distorted scale of values of its citizens produce the internal conditions that make external conquest inevitable. The phrase "being cursed is the believer" establishes that the believer is the determining factor in the client nation. As goes the believer, so goes the client nation to God.

B. Trusting in People vs. Trusting in the Lord

The tumbleweed believer is characterized by putting confidence in people rather than in the Lord. This does not refer to reasonable reliance on human institutions or legitimate authorities — it refers to the mental attitude that looks to a human being as the source of deliverance in times of crisis. When millions of believers express profound disappointment over the political decisions of candidates, or place their hope for national recovery in the election of a particular person, they are operating as tumbleweed believers. The issue is not political engagement but the orientation of the soul: the tumbleweed believer has substituted human solutions for divine solutions.

C. Making the Sin Nature the Source of Strength

The phrase "makes the sin nature his strength" describes the operational result of negative volition toward Bible doctrine. The Hebrew noun lev (לֵב), translated "heart," refers not to the physiological heart but to the right lobe of the soul — the location of the stream of consciousness where thought circulates. When the heart "turns away from the Lord," this describes the volitional decision to reject metabolized Bible doctrine as the source of thinking, norms, standards, and problem-solving capacity. With the right lobe emptied of divine viewpoint, the sin nature becomes the default source of strength — the believer operates from the arrogance complex, the emotional complex, and the lust pattern rather than from the problem-solving devices of the spiritual life.

Battle of Defense Mechanisms Denial, projection, and other defense mechanisms create garbage in the subconscious that corrupts the stream of consciousness.

E. The Tumbleweed Analogy

The tumbleweed has no root system. It is detached from any source of nourishment and stability. Whatever direction the wind blows, the tumbleweed goes. This is the perfect analogy for the believer without metabolized Bible doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness. Without doctrinal roots, there is no foundation, no ability to think in terms of divine viewpoint, no stability when circumstances change. The tumbleweed believer changes position with every new wind of doctrine, every new political movement, every new emotional appeal. The result is stated in unambiguous terms: "He will not see prosperity when it comes." The prosperity in view is spiritual prosperity — capacity for life from Bible doctrine. The tumbleweed believer has no capacity to receive blessing even when blessing is available. He lives in "dried-up places in the desert, a land of salt which is not inhabited" — a vivid picture of the spiritual wasteland that results from negative volition toward doctrine.

III. The Tree Believer: Positive Volition and Doctrinal Roots

A. Being Made Happy — The Plus-H Problem-Solving Device

The tree believer is introduced with the phrase "being made happy is the believer who trusts in the Lord." This is not a wish or a hope but a categorical statement of divine provision. The happiness in view is the problem-solving device of sharing the happiness of God (plus H) — one of the most powerful defensive positions on the flat line of the soul. This happiness is not dependent on circumstances but is produced by the filling of the Spirit and the circulation of metabolized doctrine in the stream of consciousness. It functions as a flank position on the flat line, working in conjunction with personal love for God and occupation with the person of Jesus Christ to provide maximum defensive capability against the outside pressures of adversity and prosperity.

B. The Faith-Rest Drill: Batak vs. Amen

The Hebrew verb batak (בָּטַח), translated "trusts," is distinct from the Hebrew verb amen (אָמַן) used for salvation faith. The hiphil stem of amen describes the faith-perception that results in regeneration — as when Abraham believed in the Lord and it was credited to his account for righteousness (Genesis 15:6). The verb batak describes the faith-rest drill — the post-salvation operational mechanism by which the believer mixes the promises of God with faith and applies doctrinal rationales to the circumstances of life. This is the third battalion on the flat line of the soul, positioned as an instant reaction force to prevent the outside pressures of adversity and prosperity from being converted into the inside pressure of stress in the soul.

C. The Water and the Stream: Scripture and Metabolized Doctrine

The tree is planted "by the water" — water being used throughout Scripture as an analogy for the Word of God (cf. Ephesians 5:26). The water represents the content of Scripture in its written form. The "stream" represents specific categories of doctrine as they are metabolized and circulate in the stream of consciousness. From the stream, the tree "sends out roots" — this is the process of extrapolating the ten problem-solving devices from metabolized doctrine and stationing them on the flat line of the soul as the defense line of the spiritual life.

IV. The Mechanics: Spiritual Birth Demands Spiritual Life

A. The Two Births

The contrast between the tumbleweed believer and the tree believer is ultimately a contrast between believers who do not execute their spiritual life and believers who do. Both categories have experienced regeneration — both have been born again through faith in Jesus Christ. But spiritual birth creates a spiritual life that must be executed. The mechanics of this spiritual life are what Jeremiah describes in the tree analogy.

Birth one is physical birth — the emergence of the fetus from the womb, at which point God creates soul life and imputes it to biological life. The Hebrew preposition min-beten (מִבֶּטֶן) and the Greek ek koilias (ἐκ κοιλίας) both mean "out from the womb" and mark the point at which human life begins. Prior to birth, the fetus has biological life only — a genetic sin nature from the male sperm, but no soul, no volition, no human life. At birth, God creates the immaterial soul (with its volition, mentality, emotion, self-consciousness, and conscience), imputes it to biological life, and simultaneously imputes Adam's original sin to the genetically formed sin nature. The result is human life under the condition of spiritual death.

Birth two is spiritual birth — the moment of faith in Jesus Christ at which the Holy Spirit creates the human spirit and God the Father imputes His own eternal life to that human spirit. This is the new birth that Jesus described to Nicodemus: "You must be born again" — not conceived again, but born again (John 3:7). Spiritual birth produces a living human spirit indwelt by eternal life. But this spiritual birth simultaneously creates the requirement for a spiritual life to be lived. The portfolio of invisible assets given to every believer at the moment of salvation includes a unique spiritual life that only that individual believer can execute.

B. The Power System for the Spiritual Life

The tree analogy describes the power system by which the spiritual life is executed. The water represents Scripture. The stream represents metabolized doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness. The roots represent the problem-solving devices extrapolated from that doctrine and stationed on the flat line. The entire system is powered by the filling of the Holy Spirit — the indwelling Spirit who teaches doctrine and makes spiritual function possible. The mechanics are precise: (1) The Holy Spirit is the teacher of doctrine (1 Corinthians 2:12-13). (2) Doctrine moves from the left lobe (academic knowledge, gnosis) to the right lobe (metabolized knowledge, epignosis) through the grace apparatus for perception. (3) Metabolized doctrine circulates in the stream of consciousness and is extrapolated into problem-solving devices. (4) The problem-solving devices are stationed on the flat line of the soul as the defensive perimeter against outside pressures.

V. The Pivot: Spiritual Dynamics and National Impact

The tree believers in any generation form what Scripture calls the pivot — the body of mature believers whose adjustment to the justice of God sustains divine blessing on the client nation. The pivot concept applies specifically to client nations to God (priest nations in the Old Testament, the United States as a Gentile client nation in the Church Age). When the pivot is large — perhaps 8-10% of believers in the nation reaching spiritual maturity — God blesses the client nation. When the pivot shrinks below a critical threshold, the client nation moves through the five cycles of discipline toward historical destruction.

Jeremiah's generation refused to form a pivot. His peers rejected his message. The prophets taught false doctrine. The princes wanted him imprisoned and executed. The kings, though some knew he was right, were too weak to reverse the national trend. But the next generation — the teenagers and young adults who began to listen — formed a pivot that survived the fifth cycle of discipline and became the nucleus for a future restoration of Israel. These young believers took in doctrine under Jeremiah's teaching, sent out roots in the form of problem-solving devices, and went through the Babylonian death march with their leaves still green, still bearing fruit, still testifying to the reality of their God.

The spiritual dynamics described in Jeremiah 17:5-8 are not merely individual; they have national implications. The greatest responsibility of the believer in times of national adversity is not political activism but spiritual growth. The believer who takes in doctrine daily, who maintains the filling of the Spirit, who stations the problem-solving devices on the flat line of the soul, who continues to produce divine good regardless of national circumstances — that believer is contributing to the pivot that sustains divine blessing on the client nation. This is the testimony to angels. This is the impact of the individual believer on human history.

Lesson Thirteen

2 Tim. 3:4-5 | Psa. 56:3-4 — The Pattern of the Loser; The Four Battles in the Soul; Adversity vs. Stress; The Emotional Complex of Sins; The Faith Rest Drill

This lesson continues the Spiritual Life Basics series by establishing the pattern of the loser — the believer who fails to utilize the divine assets provided at salvation. The focus scripture from 2 Timothy 3:4-5 describes individuals who possess an outward form of the spiritual life but deny its power. They are characterized by arrogance, emotional instability, and preference for pleasure over divine reality. Understanding how believers become losers requires examining the four battles in the soul and the distinction between adversity (outside pressure) and stress (inside pressure). The lesson culminates in an exposition of Psalm 56:3-4, which demonstrates how the faith rest drill stops the conversion of fear into soul-destroying stress.

I. The Four Battles in the Soul

The spiritual life operates within the soul, and the soul is a battleground. The right lobe of the soul (the heart in biblical terminology) functions as the command post. The left lobe handles academic perception. The stream of consciousness in the right lobe contains seven compartments where metabolized doctrine circulates. The subconscious serves as a repository — ideally for stored doctrine, but in the failing believer, for accumulated garbage. From metabolized doctrine in the stream of consciousness come the ten problem-solving devices that form the FLOT line (forward line of troops) of the soul.

The FLOT line is the believer's defensive perimeter. It protects the soul from the outside pressures of adversity and prosperity. When the FLOT line is manned with problem-solving devices, outside pressure remains outside. When the FLOT line is undefended, outside pressure breaks through and becomes stress in the soul — and stress is always sin nature control.

The Four Battles Defined

There are four distinct battles that occur in the soul of the believer:

1. The Battle of Stress in the Soul. This is the foundational battle. Outside pressures of adversity or prosperity attack the FLOT line. If the problem-solving devices are not in place, the pressure breaks through and becomes stress. Stress is tantamount to old sin nature control of the soul.

2. The Battle of the Soul Sins. Once stress has entered the soul, the believer becomes vulnerable to the arrogance complex and the emotional complex of sins. These mental attitude sins compound the damage and accelerate spiritual decline.

3. The Battle of False Doctrine. When the soul is dominated by arrogance and emotion, a vacuum opens in the right lobe. This vacuum draws in false doctrine, human viewpoint, and evil. The believer's thinking becomes progressively contaminated.

4. The Battle of Defense Mechanisms. This is the most destructive battle. Defense mechanisms are psychological strategies the soul employs to cope with unbearable pressure. While they may provide temporary survival value (especially for victims of childhood trauma), they produce massive garbage in both the subconscious and the stream of consciousness. Defense mechanisms battle and destroy the application of metabolized doctrine to experience.

II. Outside Pressure: Adversity and Prosperity

Outside pressure comes in two forms: adversity and prosperity. Both attack the FLOT line of the soul. Both can be converted into stress if the problem-solving devices are not operational.

Adversity is any suffering or pressure that originates outside the soul. The eight categories include:

The capacity to be alone without being lonely is a byproduct of spiritual maturity. If a believer can entertain himself in a normal, constructive way without constant external stimulation, he has capacity for life. If he cannot, he will be his own worst enemy.

Each form of lust amplifies the outside pressure and increases the likelihood of breakthrough into stress.

III. The Critical Distinction: Adversity vs. Stress

Three principles govern the relationship between adversity and stress:

Principle 1: Adversity is always an outside pressure. It is what circumstances do to you. Adversity may be unfair. Adversity may be unjust. This world is filled with injustice — there are as many injustices in life as there are sin natures multiplied exponentially. This is the devil's world.

Principle 2: Stress is what you do to yourself. Stress is an inside pressure of the soul. When stress is present, there is only one person to blame: yourself. The believer may blame circumstances, blame others who apply pressure, blame those who manipulate or disappoint. But stress cannot be blamed on anyone but the believer who allows the conversion. Until the believer takes responsibility for using volition to create stress, there will be no spiritual growth.

Principle 3: Adversity is inevitable; stress is optional. Every believer will face a certain amount of adversity in life. Whether that adversity is converted into stress depends entirely on the believer — specifically, on whether the problem-solving devices are functioning on the FLOT line of the soul.

Stress is sin nature control of the soul. Stress establishes the pattern for failure in the spiritual life. When stress takes hold, the believer enters the eight stages of reversionism: reaction and distraction regarding Bible doctrine, frantic search for happiness, emotional revolt of the soul, and progressive spiritual decline.

IV. Defense Mechanisms: Survival Becomes Destruction

Defense mechanisms deserve special attention because they represent the battle that destroys the believer. When the other battles are lost — when stress dominates, when soul sins proliferate, when false doctrine enters — defense mechanisms become the soul's desperate attempt to cope.

The tragedy of defense mechanisms is that they serve different functions at different life stages. In childhood, especially for victims of abuse, defense mechanisms may be the only means of psychological survival. The child who dissociates during abuse, who escapes mentally to survive physically, is using a defense mechanism to stay alive. But the same mechanism that enables survival in childhood becomes a source of destruction in adulthood.

Defense mechanisms produce massive garbage in the soul. They fill the subconscious with accumulated trash. They litter the stream of consciousness with debris that blocks the circulation of metabolized doctrine. They battle and destroy the application of doctrine to experience. When defense mechanisms take over the soul, the believer has lost the critical battle.

Yet as long as the believer is alive, this battle can still be won. Recovery is possible. The believer can still be filled with the Spirit. The believer can still metabolize Bible doctrine even when loaded with accumulated garbage. That metabolized doctrine can be pushed into the stream of consciousness. It can drive the garbage out. The mechanics of recovery involve the same grace apparatus for perception that operates in any believer — it simply operates against greater resistance.

V. Psalm 56:3-4: The Faith Rest Drill in Action

Psa. 56:3-4 “When I am afraid, I put my trust in you. In God, whose word I praise, in God I trust; I shall not be afraid. What can flesh do to me?” (ESV)

This passage is a five-line Hebrew poem that demonstrates the faith rest drill stopping fear at the FLOT line. The structure moves from fear to faith to fearlessness — a complete cycle of spiritual victory over the emotional complex of sins.

Line-by-Line Analysis

Line 1: "When I am afraid." This is the outside pressure of adversity being converted into stress. Fear is the first category of the emotional complex of sins. The psalmist honestly acknowledges the reality of fear — he does not deny the pressure.

Line 2: "I will put my trust in you." Here is the faith rest drill engaging at the FLOT line. The third battalion on the FLOT line is the faith rest drill — mixing the promises of God with faith, employing the doctrinal rationales, moving through the three stages. When the third battalion is in place, the attack stops at the FLOT line.

Line 3: "In God, whose word I praise." This reveals the foundation: doctrinal orientation. Bible doctrine has number one priority in the soul. If doctrine does not have priority, the believer will never achieve consistency. Metabolized doctrine forms the personnel for the battalion. The word of God is the source of the problem-solving devices.

Line 4: "In God I have put my trust." This is the response of occupation with the person of Christ — the tenth battalion on the FLOT line, the heavy battalion. The faith rest drill holds the line; occupation with Christ delivers the flanking counterattack. This is single envelopment: hold and hit.

Line 5: "I shall not be afraid." The conclusion. The psalmist began with fear; he ends without fear. The problem-solving devices have done their work. The outside pressure was stopped at the FLOT line. Fear was not converted into stress.

The Principle of Fear Accumulation

The French neurologist Pierre Janet demonstrated that fear follows a principle of accumulation: the more things surrendered to fear, the more things feared. The extent of surrender to fear determines the capacity for fear. The greater the capacity for fear, the greater the power of fear in life. When the believer lacks the problem-solving devices, fear breaks through the FLOT line and becomes stress — and this is precisely how the believer becomes a loser in the spiritual life.

VI. The Volition Factor

The battle in the soul always returns to volition. The old sin nature and the Holy Spirit are in perpetual conflict within the believer's soul. The outcome depends on which one the believer empowers through volition.

A missionary teaching among Native American believers struggled to communicate this concept until he used an illustration from their experience. He said: "The Holy Spirit is like a white dog. The sin nature is like a black dog. They are always fighting in your soul." One listener asked the critical question: "Which one wins?" The answer: "The one you say 'sic 'em' to."

This captures the volition factor perfectly. The believer who empowers the sin nature through volition becomes a loser. The believer who empowers the Holy Spirit through volition becomes a winner. There is no neutrality. Every moment of every day, the believer is either advancing in the spiritual life or declining. The direction is determined by volition.

6. The principle of fear accumulation. The more things surrendered to fear, the more things feared. The greater the capacity for fear, the greater the power of fear in life. The believer without problem-solving devices converts fear into stress — and this is how the believer becomes a loser in the spiritual life.

7. The volition factor. The battle in the soul returns to volition. The old sin nature and the Holy Spirit are in perpetual conflict. The outcome depends on which one the believer empowers through volition. There is no neutrality — every moment is either advance or decline.

Lesson Fourteen

Rom. 8:28 — The Prerequisite of Love for God; The Law of Volitional Responsibility; Recovery through Rebound

Rom. 8:28 “And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good, for those who are called according to his purpose.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: We know that to those who love God, He causes all things to work together for good, to those who are the called according to His purpose.

This lesson examines the prerequisite condition embedded in Romans 8:28 — the requirement of love for God — and demonstrates how claiming promises without meeting their conditions produces failure rather than faith-rest victory. The lesson then addresses the profile of the Christian loser, the two laws governing self-induced misery and divine discipline, and the grace procedure for recovery through the rebound technique.

I. The Prerequisite of Romans 8:28

Romans 8:28 is frequently quoted as a blanket assurance that all circumstances will resolve favorably for the believer. The corrected translation, however, reveals a critical prerequisite: 'to those who love God, He causes all things to work together for good.' The verb is a causative active — God is the subject who causes the favorable outcome, not 'all things' as the King James rendering suggests. The promise is conditioned upon the believer's love for God.

A. Personal Love for God as a Doctrinal Attainment

Love for God is not a sentimental emotion but a doctrinal attainment produced through the consistent intake of Bible doctrine. The believer who is preoccupied with personal problems, emotional reactions, and social interactions has no capacity for genuine love toward God. Personal love for God presupposes that the believer has advanced through the problem-solving devices and stationed the heavy battalions on the forward line of defense in the soul.

B. The Structure of Divine Love

God possesses both personal love and impersonal love. His personal love is directed toward objects of equivalent righteousness — God the Father's personal love extends to God the Son and God the Holy Spirit, who possess co-equal, co-eternal, and co-infinite righteousness. In personal love, the merit resides in the object.

God's impersonal love is directed toward mankind in spiritual death. John 3:16 states that God loved the world — a world of spiritually dead individuals possessing no righteousness acceptable to God. In impersonal love, the merit resides in the subject. God's perfect righteousness and justice constitute the virtue that enables Him to love the unlovable. This impersonal love motivated the plan of salvation and the substitutionary work of Christ on the cross.

John 3:16 “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: For God loved the world so much that He gave His Son, the uniquely born one, that whosoever believes in Him should never perish, but have eternal life.
Rom. 5:8 “but God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: God demonstrates His love toward us in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died as a substitute for us.

C. Divine Self-Esteem and the Absence of Threat

God the Father never felt threatened by planning salvation. God the Son never felt threatened by receiving the imputation and judgment of the sins of the world on the cross. God the Holy Spirit never feels threatened by making the gospel perspicuous to spiritually dead humanity. Divine self-esteem — the perfect love that each member of the Godhead has for His own perfect righteousness — eliminates all sense of threat. This is the love that God shares with Church Age believers who advance to maturity.

D. Impersonal Love for All Mankind

When the believer develops impersonal love for all mankind — a love that depends on the believer's own virtue rather than the merit of the object — external hostility, ridicule, hatred, or even admiration cannot penetrate the forward line of defense. This impersonal love is a component of the happiness that God shares with the mature believer. It creates a flank protection that adversity and prosperity cannot breach.

E. Claiming Promises with Prerequisites

Not all promises in Scripture are unconditional. Romans 8:28 contains a conditional clause: 'to those who love God.' A believer who has not advanced to the point of genuine love for God cannot claim this promise and expect it to function. Attempting to claim Romans 8:28 while dominated by emotional sins, arrogance, and irrationality produces frustration and the false conclusion that the faith-rest drill does not work. The believer must distinguish between promises with prerequisites and promises that apply universally to all believers in fellowship.

II. The Profile of the Christian Loser

Christian losers are defined as born-again believers who use their own volition to convert the outside pressure of adversity into the inside pressure of stress in the soul, or who convert the outside pressure of prosperity into internal stress. Scripture provides seven descriptions of this condition.

A. Seven Descriptions of the Christian Loser

1. The believer who has drifted off course from grace (Gal. 5:4). The Greek term is navigational — the believer without a compass (Bible doctrine) has gone off course. This is not loss of salvation but loss of orientation to God's grace policy.

2. The believer who has come short of the grace of God (Heb. 12:15). This believer has entered into defense mechanisms — repression, denial, and projection — and is divorced from reality.

3. The lukewarm believer who is neither hot nor cold (Rev. 3:15–16). Cold represents the unbeliever; hot represents the believer who is growing in grace. The lukewarm believer is saved but spiritually inert.

4. The tormented soul (2 Pet. 2:7–8). Lot is the illustration — a believer who placed himself in an environment of constant moral assault, was tormented daily, yet made no effort to leave. He was under the law of volitional responsibility: he did it to himself.

5. The shipwrecked believer (1 Tim. 1:19). This believer has rejected faith and a good conscience, resulting in spiritual catastrophe.

6. The neurotic or psychotic believer (James 1:8; 4:8; Deut. 28). The Greek dipsuchos (δίψυχος) — double-souled — describes instability and fragmentation. The Hebrew shigga'on describes madness resulting from sustained negative volition.

7. The flawed believer (James 1:23–24). This believer lives in a state of denial, having heard doctrine and immediately forgotten it, taking no action.

B. The Christian Loser Contradicts the Protocol Plan of God

The Christian loser cannot lose salvation — the doctrine of eternal security remains intact. However, such a believer contradicts God's plan for his life. In this state, the believer grieves the Holy Spirit (Eph. 4:30), quenches the Holy Spirit (1 Thess. 5:19), is an enemy of the cross (Phil. 3:18), and is classified as an enemy of God (James 4:4). A perfect plan designed for imperfect persons is being rejected through negative volition.

III. The Two Laws Governing Self-Induced Misery and Divine Discipline

Two distinct laws govern the consequences of negative volition in the believer's life. Understanding these laws eliminates confusion about the source of suffering.

A. The Law of Volitional Responsibility

The law of volitional responsibility states: you do it to yourself. When adversity breaks through the forward line of defense and becomes stress in the soul, the believer has no one to blame but himself. This is not divine discipline — it is the natural consequence of negative volition and failure to deploy the problem-solving devices.

Hos. 8:7a “For they sow the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: They sow to the wind and reap the whirlwind.
Gal. 6:7–8 “Do not be deceived: God is not mocked, for whatever one sows, that will he also reap. For the one who sows to his own flesh will from the flesh reap corruption, but the one who sows to the Spirit will from the Spirit reap eternal life.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: Be not deceived, God is not mocked; whatever a person sows, that will he also reap. For the one who sows to the sin nature shall from the flesh reap corruption.

The believer who attributes all suffering to satanic attack or divine testing may be evading the primary cause: his own bad decisions. The law of volitional responsibility accounts for the majority of believer misery.

B. The Law of Divine Punitive Action

The law of divine punitive action involves discipline administered by the Supreme Court of Heaven, where the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit preside as judges. Divine discipline is administered in three progressive stages:

1. Warning discipline (Rev. 3:20) — the initial phase designed to alert the believer to his deviation.

2. Intensive discipline (Heb. 12:6) — escalated discipline applied when warning discipline is ignored.

3. Dying discipline (1 John 5:16) — the sin unto death, the terminal stage of divine discipline for the believer who refuses to recover.

Prov. 3:12 “for the LORD reproves him whom he loves, as a father the son in whom he delights.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: For whom the Lord loves, He disciplines, even as a father the son in whom he delights.

C. Both Laws in a Single Passage

Prov. 22:8 “Whoever sows injustice will reap calamity, and the rod of his fury will fail.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: He who sows wickedness shall reap trouble (law of volitional responsibility), and the rod of his wrath shall be ready (law of divine punitive action).
Col. 3:25 “For the wrongdoer will be paid back for the wrong he has done, and there is no partiality.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: He who does wrong will receive the consequences of that wrong which he has done (law of volitional responsibility), and there is no partiality (law of divine punitive action).

IV. Recovery through Rebound: 1 John 1:9

1 John 1:9 “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: If we cite our sins, He is faithful and righteous to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

A. The Meaning of Homologeo

The verse begins with a third-class conditional clause: 'if we cite' — the Greek verb homologeo (ὁμολογέω). This verb originated in the Attic Greek legal system of fifth-century Athens. In a courtroom, homologeo referred to citing a legal precedent in one's favor. It does not mean 'to say the same thing as God says' in some abstract sense — it means to cite the only courtroom case that favors the sinning believer: the judgment of all sins at the cross.

B. The Courtroom Case in Our Favor

Every sin committed by every believer — past, present, and future — was imputed to Christ on the cross and judged. Our sins went to court when Christ received the imputation and judgment of the sins of the world. When He cried, 'Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani' — 'My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?' — the perfect humanity of Christ was bearing the judicial penalty for all human sin. When the judgment was complete, He declared 'Tetelestai' — 'It is finished.' The perfect tense indicates that the work was completed in the past with results that stand finished forever. This is the legal precedent the believer cites when naming sins to God.

C. The Mechanics of the Verb

The present tense of homologeo is a gnomic present expressing a universal truth — a state or condition that perpetually exists as a grace provision for recovery from sin nature control.

The active voice indicates that the sinful, carnal believer produces the action of citing sins in the privacy of his priesthood. Every believer is a priest and represents himself before God.

The subjunctive mood is the marker of the third-class condition: maybe the believer will cite his sins, and maybe he will not. The potential is qualified by the element of contingency — the believer's volition determines whether recovery occurs.

D. What Rebound Does Not Include

The rebound technique excludes all human works and emotional performances:

1. No penance — penance is works, not grace.

2. No emphasis on how the believer feels about his sins — how the believer feels is irrelevant; how God feels about sin is the issue, and He judged every sin at the cross.

3. No auto-flagellation or promises to do better — making promises to God while out of fellowship adds another sin on top of the sins being named. Promising to attend prayer meetings, read the Bible more, or improve behavior negates the grace principle.

4. No public confession — the believer represents himself before God in the privacy of his priesthood.

5. No begging, pleading, or emotional displays — these are arrogance and emotional sin, not grace orientation.

E. The Result: Reestablishing the Forward Line of Defense

When the believer cites his sins to God — fulfilling the non-meritorious condition of simply naming known sins — the filling of the Holy Spirit is restored. The first battalion (rebound) and the second battalion (filling of the Spirit) are now stationed on the forward line of defense. From this restored position, the believer can begin to rebuild the entire defensive perimeter through the intake of Bible doctrine.

Lesson Fifteen

1 John 1:9 — ὁμολογέω (homologeō), ἀφίημι (aphiēmi), δίκαιος (dikaios) — The Rebound Technique and Recovery from the Emotional Complex of Sins

1 John 1:9 “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: If we name [cite, acknowledge] our sins, He is faithful and righteous, with the result that He cancels our sins and purifies us from all wrongdoing.

The rebound technique stands as the first operational gate in the divine dynasphere. No element of the spiritual life functions apart from recovery through 1 John 1:9. The four battles of the soul — stress, sin, false doctrine, and defense mechanisms — all require rebound as the initial recovery mechanism. This lesson examines the mechanics of the rebound technique, the theological basis for divine forgiveness, and the devastating categories of emotional sinning that necessitate constant application of the grace provision.

I. The Four Battles of the Soul

The spiritual life operates on a flot line established by the first battalion — rebound. Every battle of the soul requires this technique for recovery. The believer faces four distinct battlefields where the old sin nature seeks control:

Battle Description
First Battle Stress in the soul — external pressures of adversity or prosperity become internal pressures
Second Battle Sin in the soul — primarily the sins of the soul rather than overt sins
Third Battle False doctrine in the soul — concepts that distort biblical truth
Fourth Battle Defense mechanisms of the soul — the battle that produces psychopathology in believers

The fourth battle produces what Scripture calls the non-compassionate believer — one whose defense mechanisms have overridden rational function. These battles interconnect; failure in one area accelerates failure in others. Rebound remains the singular recovery mechanism for all four battlefields.

II. Exegesis of 1 John 1:9 — The Verb Homologeō

The verb homologeō (ὁμολογέω) means to cite, name, admit, or acknowledge. The term carries legal connotations: to cite a courtroom case in your favor. The courtroom case cited is always the work of Jesus Christ on the cross, where all sins were imputed to Him and judged.

This verb contains absolutely no merit as far as the subject is concerned. The merit resides entirely in the object — the finished work of Christ. Feeling sorry for sin is not the issue. Lordship is not the issue. Commitment is not the issue. The only issue is Christ. This principle governs salvation ('Whosoever believeth in him shall never perish, but have eternal life') and equally governs post-salvation recovery.

Grammatical Analysis

The verb homologeō appears as a gnomic present, active voice, potential subjunctive. The gnomic present indicates a universal, timeless principle that applies without exception. The active voice places the action squarely on the believer's volition. The potential subjunctive implies future reference from the moment of sinning and introduces the element of contingency — the believer may or may not utilize the provision.

Two Categories of Post-Salvation Sins

Believers commit two categories of sins. First, unknown sins — sins committed without awareness that the action constitutes sin. Second, sins of cognizance — sins committed with full awareness. Both categories fall under the law of volitional responsibility. The believer wanted to do it and used volition to commit the act. Even claiming ignorance provides no exemption; volition was exercised in both cases. The field of hamartiology — the theological study of sin — addresses not only the sin nature and its control of the soul but also the volitional responsibility of the individual.

III. Divine Faithfulness and Righteousness

God responds to the believer's confession in two ways: He is faithful, and He is righteous. These two attributes of divine essence guarantee the invariable outcome of the rebound technique.

Faithfulness — Invariable Response

Divine faithfulness means God always does exactly the same thing. No matter how obnoxious the sins may be, no matter how others may despise the believer for his failures, God always forgives those sins without exception. No matter how the individual is traumatized by his own sins, no matter how much trauma exists in the peripheral area of those sins, the divine response remains constant. Faithfulness and righteousness go together in the essence of God. There is no silliness in the essence of God, no vindictiveness. To suggest otherwise is blasphemous and unthinkable.

Righteousness — The Basis for Forgiveness

The Greek adjective dikaios (δίκαιος) means righteous, not merely just. God possesses perfect righteousness — one half of His divine holiness. The other half is divine justice. At the moment of salvation, one of the forty things given to the believer is the imputed righteousness of God. A pipeline exists between the righteousness of God in the believer and the justice of God. Righteousness demands righteousness; what the righteousness of God demands, the justice of God executes.

This principle explains logistical grace support. All logistical grace flows from the justice of God down the pipeline to the righteousness of God. God sustains both winners and losers among believers. Losers often dramatize this principle — observers recognize that certain believers 'don't deserve it' and expect immediate divine punishment. Yet blessing continues because both winners and losers possess the imputed righteousness of God.

The Pipeline Principle

God found a way to bless believers without compromising His essence. If God blessed even one believer because of something that believer had done, then God is not God. The justice of God, as one half of divine holiness, responds always to the righteousness of God. All Christian production and Christian service exist as a result of spiritual growth, not as the basis for receiving blessing. No one is ever blessed because of giving, tithing, or any form of Christian activity. All blessing comes because the believer possesses the imputed righteousness of God.

IV. The Verb Aphiēmi — Cancellation of Sins

The result clause introduces the verb aphiēmi (ἀφίημι), an aorist active subjunctive. This verb literally means to cancel. Because the sins are canceled, they are forgiven. The reason they are canceled has nothing to do with anything the believer has done.

There are no strings on divine forgiveness. When the believer rebounds from the privacy of the priesthood, that is the end of the matter. No promises to God are required. No groveling is necessary. No system of penance, no auto-flagellation. All such additions cloud the issue and indicate failure to understand the most basic principle of the spiritual life. Paul declares in Colossians chapter 2 that those sins were nailed to the cross.

Grammatical Analysis

The aorist tense is a gnomic aorist for a dogmatic statement of Bible doctrine. This statement of doctrine is so fixed in certainty, so axiomatic in character, that it is described as a fact which must be accepted by the believer. The active voice indicates that God the Father in every case produces the action of the verb on the basis of the salvation work of Jesus Christ on the cross. The subjunctive mood is a potential subjunctive, implying future reference from the post-salvation sins and qualified by the element of contingency.

Keeping Short Accounts

The believer who keeps short accounts — rebounding immediately at the point of soul sins before entering the lust pattern, trends, and the downward spiral of reversionism — may never even experience divine discipline. Extended carnality, however, builds up repercussions that must die gradually. If the believer has progressed to dying discipline, the problem compounds exponentially.

V. Purification and Restoration to the Divine Dynasphere

The final clause states that God purifies us apo (ἀπό) — out from, away from — all adikia (ἀδικία), wrongdoing or unrighteousness. This ablative of separation indicates complete removal. The sins of ignorance are included in this purification.

Purification restores the believer to function inside the divine dynasphere. The believer can only live inside this operational sphere when purified. Gate 1 is the filling of the Spirit. Gate 2 is basic doctrine. Gate 3 involves enforced and genuine humility, objectivity, and teachability. Gate 4 enables continued spiritual growth and momentum. Gate 5 is spiritual self-esteem. Gate 6 is spiritual autonomy. Gate 7 is the valley of testing leading to Gate 8 — spiritual maturity, the high ground, the point of fulfilling the protocol plan of God.

Suffering After Rebound

Two outcomes are possible regarding suffering after rebound. First, God may immediately cancel all suffering related to the sin pattern. Second, the suffering may continue if the believer has yet to take responsibility for personal failures — an essential component of spiritual maturity. When divine discipline continues after rebound, it transforms into suffering for blessing, designed to accelerate the growth lost during the period out of fellowship.

VI. The Eight Stages of Reversionism

When the believer fails to rebound and continues in carnality, the sin nature assumes control of the soul. The lust pattern becomes extremely active, producing a trend toward either self-righteous arrogance (legalism) or antinomianism. Entry into either gate initiates the eight stages of reversionism:

Stage Description
Stage 1 Distraction — attention diverted from doctrine
Stage 2 Frantic search for happiness — pursuit of happiness through wrong sources
Stage 3 Operation boomerang — the pursuit fails; happiness remains elusive
Stage 4 Warning discipline — 'Behold I stand at the door and knock'
Stage 5 Locked-in negative volition — emotional revolt of the soul; neurosis develops
Stage 6 Blackout of the soul — all metabolized doctrine is forgotten
Stage 7 Scar tissue of the soul — hardening that blocks any future doctrinal reception
Stage 8 Reverse process reversionism — Christian moral or immoral degeneracy

At any stage, recovery through rebound remains available. Like the prodigal son — a born-again believer who strayed — the believer may come to the end of the rope and remember grace orientation. The prodigal was a son when the story began and a son when it ended. His permanent relationship with the father was never in jeopardy. The recovery principle applies regardless of how far the believer has descended into reversionism.

VII. Old Testament Precedent — Psalm 32:5

Psalm 32:5 “I acknowledged my sin to you, and I did not cover my iniquity; I said, 'I will confess my transgressions to the LORD,' and you forgave the iniquity of my sin.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: I acknowledged my sin to You; my iniquity I did not hide from God. And I said, 'I will confess my transgression to the LORD,' and You forgave the iniquity of my sin.

David's recovery following his adultery with Bathsheba and murder of Uriah demonstrates the rebound principle in the Old Testament. David received a fourfold discipline from God that did not terminate until the Absalom rebellion. The first installment was the death of the child born from the illicit union. When the child died, David rose, washed, and resumed normal activity. His explanation: 'The child has gone to be with the Lord. The child will never come to me on earth, but I will go to the child in heaven.'

This statement affirms that children who die before reaching accountability automatically go to heaven. The doctrine of unlimited atonement — all sins of the world judged on the cross — frees the justice of God to save those who have not reached the point of positive or negative volition toward God-consciousness. David and Bathsheba later had four children, the eldest being Solomon and the youngest being Nathan. The line of Solomon leads to Joseph, but the Jeconiah curse prevented Joseph from being the genetic father of Christ. The virgin birth fulfilled the Jeconiah curse. Luke's genealogy traces Mary's line through Nathan, circumventing the curse — an illustration of cursing turned to blessing.

VIII. Standard Operating Procedure for Rebound

The procedure for the rebound technique involves three steps:

Step Action
Step 1 Recognize the problem — identify the post-salvation sinning, whether from the emotional complex or arrogance complex
Step 2 Utilize the solution — apply the rebound technique immediately
Step 3 Emphasize biblical priorities — God-emphasis over people-emphasis; Bible doctrine as the number one priority

The sin nature's control of the soul always begins with motivation corrupted by sins of the soul, not with overt sin. Recognition of the problem requires awareness that the starting point is internal, not external. Following rebound, the believer must continue growing in grace through metabolized doctrine in the stream of consciousness.

IX. The Emotional Complex of Sins

The emotional complex constitutes one of the great enemies of the spiritual life. While legitimate emotion exists as a responder to truth, sinful emotion is reaction. There is no power in emotion. Power resides in doctrine. Emotion is either a responder (good emotion) or a reactor (sinful emotion). Five categories of emotional sinning devastate the believer's spiritual life:

Category One — Hysteria

This category includes fear, worry, anxiety, panic, and consternation. Irrationality characterizes every form of emotional sinning. Irrationality leads to dissociation, which fills the subconscious with garbage that leaks into the stream of consciousness.

Moses warned in Deuteronomy 31:8: 'Do not fear, do not become unraveled.' The Hebrew text uses a term that means to come apart, become unraveled. This describes precisely what happens with emotional sins — the soul becomes unraveled and irrationality takes over. Isaiah 41:10 states: 'Fear not, for I am with you; be not dismayed, for I am your God.' Paul wrote to Timothy (2 Timothy 1:7): 'God has not given us a lifestyle of fear, but of power and of virtue love and of sound judgment.' Fear and power are antithetical — the believer has either the power of the filling of the Spirit or the sin of fear.

Category Two — Hatred

Anger and hatred combine with the arrogance complex to produce irrationality. This category includes bitter jealousy, loathing, animosity, vulnerability to imagined insult or injury, implacability, malice (the intense desire to injure someone), tantrums, irrational violence, and even murder.

Category Three — Self-Centeredness

Arrogant self-righteousness produces egotistical irrationality. The self-centered believer becomes insensitive about self but hypersensitive about others. This category results in projection, denial, self-pity, whining, and the persistent refusal to take responsibility for personal failures, always finding someone else to blame.

Category Four — Reaction

The reaction category combines arrogance sins with emotional sins. Jealousy becomes irrational. Bitterness becomes irrational. Vindictiveness becomes compulsive. The strong desire to hurt and destroy leads to revenge motivation and revenge modus operandi. Vilification is a primary expression of this category.

No one has been given the right to sit on the supreme court of heaven with God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit. Yet every act of gossip, slander, maligning, or judgment usurps that divine prerogative. Matthew 7:1-2 describes triple compound discipline: the believer is disciplined for the motivation (arrogance and emotional sins), for the act of vituperation itself, and for whatever sins are named in the vilification, since those sins have discipline assigned to them. The result is triple misery — and it is never worth it.

Category Five — Guilt

Guilt is a feeling of responsibility or remorse for some real or imagined offense. Biblically defined, guilt belongs to the emotional complex of sins. It becomes morbid self-reproach, emotional feelings of culpability for imagined offenses or inculcated offenses (when someone else has made you feel you are wrong when you may not be). Guilt may also arise from arrogant preoccupation with the correctness of one's behavior — self-righteous arrogance.

Guilt relates to repression, a defense mechanism. Repression is reaction from the stream of consciousness regarding painful or disagreeable ideas, memories, feelings, or impulses. Repression leads to denial. Denial divorces the believer from reality while accumulating garbage flaws in the subconscious. This becomes a loaded gun. When the believer engages in vituperation, these familiar personal flaws are assigned to someone else through projection — firing the gun.

Guilt becomes sin when a believer uses rebound then fails to forget those things which are behind. Guilt is caused by repression, denial, projection, and manipulation. Guilt motivates manipulation by stipulation. Without rebound, guilt becomes a motivator for the entire life, superseding metabolized doctrine. The result is irrationality and dictatorship within whatever sphere the believer can influence. Guilt causes the believer to be manipulated by legalism and controlled by self-righteous arrogance. Guilt causes people-emphasis over God-emphasis. The weak end up controlling the strong. Guilt eliminates grace orientation from the life.

Conclusions from Lesson Fifteen

1.. Rebound is the first operational gate. No element of the spiritual life functions apart from recovery through 1 John 1:9. All four battles of the soul — stress, sin, false doctrine, defense mechanisms — require rebound as the recovery mechanism.

2.. The verb homologeō means to cite a courtroom case in your favor. The case cited is always the work of Christ on the cross where all sins were imputed and judged. No merit resides in the subject; all merit is in the object.

3.. Divine faithfulness guarantees invariable response. No matter how heinous the sins, God always does exactly the same thing. Faithfulness and righteousness together in divine essence eliminate any possibility of vindictiveness or inconsistency.

4.. Divine righteousness is the basis for forgiveness. The same justice that judged sins on the cross is free to forgive those sins when cited. The righteousness of God imputed at salvation establishes the pipeline through which all blessing flows.

5.. The verb aphiēmi means cancellation. Sins are not merely forgiven; they are canceled. No strings attach to divine forgiveness — no promises required, no penance, no groveling. Those sins were nailed to the cross.

6.. Keeping short accounts prevents divine discipline. The believer who rebounds immediately at the point of soul sins, before entering the lust pattern and reversionism, may never experience discipline. Extended carnality builds repercussions that die gradually.

7.. Purification restores the believer to the divine dynasphere. Only the purified believer can function inside this operational sphere. All eight gates become accessible again following rebound.

8.. The eight stages of reversionism describe the downward spiral of carnality. Recovery through rebound remains available at any stage, as illustrated by the prodigal son whose permanent relationship with the father was never in jeopardy.

9.. The emotional complex of sins devastates the spiritual life. Five categories — hysteria, hatred, self-centeredness, reaction, guilt — produce irrationality and progressively divorce the believer from reality. There is no power in emotion; power resides in doctrine.

10.. Guilt as a motivator replaces metabolized doctrine. Without rebound, guilt fills both the subconscious and conscious with garbage, producing false motivation, manipulation, and dictatorship. Guilt causes people-emphasis over God-emphasis and eliminates grace orientation.

Lesson Sixteen

2 Tim. 3:5, 7 · 2 Pet. 1:2–8 · εὐσέβεια (eusebia) · ἐπίγνωσις (epignosis) · Grace Orientation · Tranquility of Soul

2 Tim. 3:5, 7 “having the appearance of godliness, but denying its power. Avoid such people. … always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: holding to a superficial form of the spiritual life, but having denied its power. … always learning, but never able to come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth.

This lesson opens the second increment of the study of eusebia — the biblical term for the believer's spiritual life. Building on the foundational principles introduced in 2 Timothy, the focus now shifts to 2 Peter 1:2–8, where eusebia and epignosis appear together three times each. The coupling of these two terms establishes the permanent operational relationship between the spiritual life and metabolized doctrine: eusebia describes what the spiritual life is; epignosis describes how it functions.

I. Governing Principles of the Spiritual Life

Seven foundational principles govern the believer's spiritual life. These principles summarize the doctrinal ground covered in the preceding lessons and establish the framework for understanding eusebia in 2 Peter.

First, you and you alone are the only one who can live your spiritual life. No one else can live it for you. The spiritual life is a post-salvation experience, operative only for the believer in the Lord Jesus Christ. You are responsible for your own decisions — good or bad, positive or negative. This demands an adult mental attitude as a Christian. Environment, childhood, rejection, injustice, or any controversy or interaction with people cannot be blamed for your own problems. Responsibility rests with the individual believer.

Second, the spiritual life was designed by God in eternity past to function under divine power only. There is no place for human power or human ability in the spiritual life. Two powers are operative: the power of God the Holy Spirit (Eph. 5:18) and the power of the Word of God (Heb. 4:12).

Third, both the filling of the Spirit and the cognition, inculcation, and metabolization of Bible doctrine are mandated for all believers. This mandate requires a change in the believer's scale of values. Sooner or later, the Word of God must be assigned number one priority. The Word of God is the revelation of His plan, His will, and His purpose for your life. The will of God cannot be known apart from Bible doctrine. Prayer without doctrine intake produces no additional knowledge of the will of God — the will of God is found in the Word of God.

Fourth, only through living your very own spiritual life, utilizing divine power, can you glorify God. Glorification of God is the fulfillment of His will, His plan, and His purpose for your life.

Fifth, the heart — the right lobe of the soul — is the location of your very own spiritual life. The spiritual life was assigned to you at the moment of salvation through faith in Christ. Though given at that moment, it was actually provided in eternity past. It is one of the forty things received at the moment of faith in Jesus Christ.

Sixth, the soul is the location of four great battles of the spiritual life. (1) The battle of stress in the soul. (2) The battle of sin in the soul. (3) The battle of false doctrine in the soul. (4) The battle of defense mechanisms in the soul. The fourth battle is the most difficult because it is the result of having been a loser in the first three.

Seventh, the fourth battle is the soul conflict of garbage versus metabolized doctrine in the stream of consciousness. When the believer is losing this battle, two categories of garbage accumulate in the soul: (1) garbage in the subconscious, related to defense mechanisms such as suppression, repression, dissociation, and denial; (2) garbage in the stream of consciousness, related to idealization, role-model arrogance, autistic fantasy, unrealistic expectation, and projection. Defense mechanisms bury garbage in the subconscious while simultaneously trashing the stream of consciousness.

II. Eusebia and Epignosis — The Operational Pair

The noun εὐσέβεια (eusebia) is consistently mistranslated in the King James Version as 'godliness' — a term that conveys no substantive meaning. The German scholar Günther Bornkamm of Heidelberg University provides the correct definition through three descriptive phrases: (1) Christian life and faith; (2) conduct in relationship to God; (3) manner of life — the spiritual life, the Christian way of life.

The noun ἐπίγνωσις (epignosis) refers to metabolized doctrine circulating in the seven compartments of the stream of consciousness — what the Bible calls the heart. The heart is the right lobe of the soul, designed not to circulate blood but to circulate doctrine. The stream of consciousness can also circulate false concepts; reality in the believer's life depends on what occupies this space.

These two words always go together in Scripture, especially in 2 Peter. Eusebia refers to the spiritual life; epignosis refers to how it functions. The spiritual life functions with Bible doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness. God has provided two ways for doctrine to be communicated: the preserved Scripture (translated into more languages than any other single volume) and the spiritual gift of pastor-teacher, which requires constant study of Scripture in the original languages.

The GAP Process

When Bible doctrine is communicated, there are two categories of listeners: those who are positive and those who are negative. For those who are positive and filled with the Holy Spirit, the Spirit teaches the human spirit and makes the doctrine understandable. At this point, the doctrine becomes pneumatikos — Bible doctrine understood academically. This academic understanding goes to the left lobe of the soul (the nous) as gnosis. This is like food on the table — it is available, but only the food that is actually eaten will benefit the person.

Metabolization occurs through positive volition — the function of the faith-rest drill. When the believer believes the doctrine understood academically, that faith transfers the doctrine from the left lobe to the right lobe. It goes to the seven compartments of the stream of consciousness and becomes epignosis. This metabolized doctrine can and will push garbage out of the stream of consciousness and eventually eliminate garbage in the subconscious — but the defense mechanisms must be addressed first.

III. Eusebia and Epignosis in 2 Peter 1:2–8

In 2 Peter 1:2–8, the word eusebia occurs three times (verses 3, 6, and 7), and epignosis occurs three times (verses 2, 3, and 8). This passage demonstrates the relationship between the spiritual life and what makes it work: epignosis — Bible doctrine metabolized and circulating in the stream of consciousness.

The Profile of the Loser (2 Tim. 3:5, 7)

The contrast is established in the two verses that form this lesson's focus passage. In 2 Timothy 3:5, believers 'hold to a superficial form of the spiritual life but have denied its power.' The superficial form includes legalism, defense mechanisms, and humanistic approaches to life — no divine viewpoint at all. The power they have denied refers to the two divine powers: the filling of the Spirit and metabolized doctrine. The result appears in 2 Timothy 3:7: these believers are 'always learning but never able to come to an epignosis knowledge of the truth.' They have gnosis (academic knowledge in the left lobe) but never epignosis (metabolized doctrine in the right lobe).

IV. The Origin of the Church Age Spiritual Life

The spiritual life of the Church Age believer originated in the dispensation of the hypostatic union. In this dispensation, Jesus Christ as eternal God also became true humanity. The principle of precedence applies: all precedent for the spiritual life began when the Lord Jesus Christ in His humanity occupied the prototype divine dynasphere.

1 Tim. 3:16 “Great indeed, we confess, is the mystery of godliness: He was manifested in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit, seen by angels, proclaimed among the nations, believed on in the world, taken up in glory.” (ESV)
Corrected translation: And by consent of all, great is the mystery of the spiritual life: the unique One was revealed in the flesh, vindicated by means of the Spirit, observed by angels, proclaimed among the nations, believed on in the world, taken up into glory.

The Prototype Flat Line

Under the filling of the Spirit, the Lord Jesus Christ established a flat line for His human soul — the operational standard for the spiritual life. The components of this flat line are visible throughout His earthly ministry:

The resurrection, ascension, and session of the Lord Jesus Christ constitute God's stamp of approval on the spiritual life of the humanity of Christ, making it the pattern for the Church Age believer.

The Greatest Spiritual Life in History

The spiritual life given to the Church Age believer is the greatest spiritual life ever given in the history of the human race. It was given under the principle of equal privilege and equal opportunity — something that never existed before in all of history. In the Old Testament, differentiation was essential. There was always a form of leadership; there was a special priesthood set apart from the people. Now there is a universal priesthood of the believer with no such separation — all have the same royal priesthood, and each one represents himself before God.

Whether you ever do anything with it is strictly up to you. The spiritual life is a means of great profit when accompanied by contentment. Contentment is plus H — sharing the happiness of God, the ninth battalion on the flat line of the soul. It means the most fantastic capacity for life: capacity for love, capacity for happiness, capacity for whatever environment you find yourself in at a given moment.

V. Grace and Peace Multiplied — 2 Peter 1:2

The second increment of the study of eusebia begins with 2 Peter 1:2. Peter opens with a phrase that provides a Bible definition of the spiritual life: 'May grace and peace be multiplied to you.'

Charis — Grace Orientation

The first nominative singular subject is χάρις (charis), translated 'grace.' It is used here for grace orientation — the fourth battalion on the flat line of the soul. Grace is what the spiritual life is all about: you don't earn it, you don't deserve it, you don't work for it. Grace flows under one system only — humility. 'God makes war against the arrogant believer, but He gives grace to the humble believer' (James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5). Grace always depends on the character of God; it never depends on our character.

Grace is never added to the believer — it is multiplied. And grace is never subtracted even from the worst believer who ever lived. This is important, encouraging, and a source of hope for all believers.

Eirēnē — Tranquility of Soul

The second nominative singular subject is εἰρήνη (eirēnē), translated 'peace.' This is not world peace but tranquility of soul from maximum metabolized doctrine circulating in the stream of consciousness. It refers to a state of soul prosperity, of soul tranquility. Part of this peace is the fact that the believer has taken metabolized doctrine and created the problem-solving devices, stationing them on a flat line. This prevents the outside pressures of adversity from becoming the inside pressures of stress in the soul — tranquility and stability of divine viewpoint.

Plēthunō — The Multiplication of Grace

The verb is πληθύνω (plēthunō), meaning 'to multiply.' It appears here as an aorist passive optative. The aorist tense is a constative aorist, summing up an action of long duration. This action began before salvation with common grace — the Holy Spirit making the gospel perspicuous to spiritual brain death. It continues through efficacious grace at the moment of faith, through the forty things God does for the believer at salvation, and extends throughout the entire Christian life to dying grace and beyond into eternity. There will never be an end to grace.

The passive voice indicates that the believer receives the action of the verb through cognition, inculcation, metabolization, and application of Bible doctrine. God multiplies grace; the believer's awareness of it comes through learning Bible doctrine. Without metabolizing Bible doctrine, the believer will never be aware of grace and will therefore have no basis for the worship function of the spiritual life.

The optative mood is a voluntative optative, expressing Peter's wish for every Church Age believer to discover and utilize the dynamics of the spiritual life. The optative mood expresses action which is subjectively possible — it recognizes that the Church Age believer must use his own volition to live the spiritual life and execute the Protocol Plan. The apostle can only communicate the necessary information; the ball is then in the court of the believer.

The Dative of Advantage

The verse concludes with a dative plural indirect object from the pronoun σύ (su), meaning 'to you.' This is a dative of advantage, connoting 'for whom something is done for their benefit.' Peter's opening statement therefore reads: 'May grace and peace — tranquility and stability of soul, in other words, living your own spiritual life — be multiplied to you for your benefit.' This is the introduction to the second stage of the study of eusebia.

Conclusions from Lesson Sixteen

1. Eusebia and epignosis form an inseparable pair. Eusebia refers to the spiritual life; epignosis refers to how it functions. The spiritual life operates with Bible doctrine — metabolized and circulating in the stream of consciousness.

2. The superficial form of the spiritual life denies divine power. Believers who hold to legalism, defense mechanisms, and humanistic approaches have denied the two powers that make the spiritual life operational: the filling of the Spirit and metabolized doctrine. They are always learning but never arriving at epignosis knowledge of the truth.

3. The Church Age spiritual life originated in the hypostatic union. Jesus Christ in His humanity occupied the prototype divine dynasphere, establishing a flat line of problem-solving devices under the filling of the Spirit. God's stamp of approval at the resurrection, ascension, and session made this the pattern for all Church Age believers.

4. The Church Age spiritual life is the greatest ever given. It was given under the principle of equal privilege and equal opportunity — something unprecedented in human history. Whether you do anything with it is strictly up to you.

5. Grace is multiplied, never added or subtracted. Grace depends on the character of God, never on the character of the believer. Even the worst believer who ever lived remains the object of grace. As long as you are alive, God has a purpose for your life: equal opportunity to execute your very own spiritual life.

6. Awareness of grace comes only through metabolized doctrine. Without metabolizing Bible doctrine, the believer will never be aware of grace and will have no basis for the worship function of the spiritual life. They that worship God must worship Him in the Spirit and in truth.

7. Peace is tranquility of soul from maximum metabolized doctrine. This tranquility cannot be penetrated by environmental adversities, tragedies, or heartaches. It is the result of the problem-solving devices stationed on the flat line of the soul, preventing outside pressure from becoming inside stress.

8. The optative mood recognizes the necessity of positive volition. Peter expresses his wish that every Church Age believer would discover and utilize the dynamics of the spiritual life. The apostle can only communicate the necessary information; the believer must use his own volition to execute the Protocol Plan.
